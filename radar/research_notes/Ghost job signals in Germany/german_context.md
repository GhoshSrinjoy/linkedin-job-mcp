## 1. Works-council mechanics (§§ 93, 95, 99 BetrVG) and works-council coverage

### Takeaway

- **What §93 BetrVG requires.** A works council (Betriebsrat, BR) can demand that vacancies be advertised internally before they are filled. Once it has demanded this, the internal posting must come before the employer's selection decision and before the §99 consent request. It cannot be made up later. A missing or deficient posting is a statutory ground to veto the hire (§99 Abs. 2 Nr. 5).
- **What the BetrVG does not require.** It never requires an external ad and does not forbid a parallel one. It gives internal applicants no priority unless that is agreed through §95 selection guidelines.
- **Coverage.** In 2024 works councils existed in only 7% of private establishments with 5+ employees, but those establishments employ 37% of private-sector employees. Coverage reaches 74% of establishments with more than 200 employees. The "internal-candidate formality" pattern is therefore a large-employer, regulated-sector phenomenon.

### Cited Findings

**Statute**

- §93: the BR can demand that "Arbeitsplätze, die besetzt werden sollen", either generally or for certain types of activity, be advertised within the establishment before they are filled. — [§ 93 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__93.html)
- §99 Abs. 1 applies "in Unternehmen mit in der Regel mehr als zwanzig wahlberechtigten Arbeitnehmern". The employer must inform the BR before every hire, present "die erforderlichen Bewerbungsunterlagen", give information on the persons involved, and obtain the BR's consent. For hires it must name the intended workplace and pay grade. — [§ 99 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__99.html)
- §99 Abs. 2 grounds for refusing consent:
  - Nr. 1: violation of law, collective agreement, works agreement, court ruling or official order.
  - Nr. 2: violation of a §95 selection guideline.
  - Nr. 3: well-founded concern that existing staff will be dismissed or otherwise disadvantaged. For permanent hires, passing over an equally suitable fixed-term employee counts as a disadvantage.
  - Nr. 4: unjustified disadvantage to the affected employee.
  - Nr. 5: the internal advertisement required under §93 was not made.
  - Nr. 6: concern about disturbance of workplace peace (e.g., racist or xenophobic conduct).
  - Source: [§ 99 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__99.html)
- §99 Abs. 3: the BR must refuse in writing, with reasons, within one week of being informed; otherwise "gilt die Zustimmung als erteilt". §99 Abs. 4: the employer may ask the labour court to replace the consent (Zustimmungsersetzung). — [§ 99 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__99.html)
- §95 Abs. 1: guidelines on personnel selection for hiring, transfers, regrading and dismissals need BR consent; if there is no agreement, the Einigungsstelle decides on the employer's application. §95 Abs. 2: "In Betrieben mit mehr als 500 Arbeitnehmern kann der Betriebsrat die Aufstellung von Richtlinien … verlangen" on professional and personal requirements and social criteria. The Einigungsstelle's ruling replaces agreement. — [§ 95 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__95.html)

**Case law**

- **BAG 11.10.2022 – 1 ABR 16/21 (timing):**
  - The employer must post internally before it decides on filling the post and before asking for consent (Leitsatz 1; Rn. 40). The statute names no fixed date (Rn. 38).
  - "Die Ausschreibung kann grundsätzlich nicht während des gerichtlichen Zustimmungsersetzungsverfahrens nachgeholt werden" (Leitsatz 2), because employees may see the outcome as already decided and not apply (Rn. 42).
  - A narrow exception was left open for sudden, unforeseeable vacancies.
  - The employer remains free in its selection and is not bound to choose internal applicants (Rn. 43).
  - The decision does not discuss parallel external recruiting or minimum posting periods.
  - Sources: [BAG 1 ABR 16/21](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/1-abr-16-21/); summary by [dbb](https://www.dbb.de/arbeitnehmende/rechtsprechung/betriebsverfassungsrecht/mitbestimmung-stellenausschreibung.html)
- **BAG 23.9.2025 – 1 ABR 19/24 (minimum content):**
  - A §93 posting must state the expected qualifications, give "eine jedenfalls schlagwortartige Bezeichnung der … Arbeitsaufgaben", and "regelmäßig auch die Angabe, mit welchem Arbeitszeitvolumen" the post is to be filled. If working time is negotiable, the posting must say so.
  - Facts: a hospital group whose BR had demanded internal posting of all vacancies (2018). A chief-physician post was advertised as full-time only. The employer chose an internal candidate, already chief physician at another clinic. The BR refused consent.
  - BAG held the deficient posting justified the refusal under Nr. 5.
  - Source: [Otto Schmidt summary, 6 Feb 2026](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/news/arbeits-und-sozialrecht/voraussetzungen-einer-innerbetrieblichen-stellenausschreibung-2026-02-06.html)
- **BAG 29.9.2020 – 1 ABR 17/19:** the duty arises only if the BR demanded it or it was agreed. It also covers positions meant for junior staff (Nachwuchskräfte). Group or company works councils (GBR/KBR) cannot restrict the local BR's §93 right. — [BAG 1 ABR 17/19](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/?p=1159101) (excerpt)
- **Temp positions:** BAG 1.2.2011 – 1 ABR 79/09 holds that §93 also covers positions to be filled permanently with temporary agency workers (Leiharbeitnehmer) — [BAG 1 ABR 79/09](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/?p=1146206) (excerpt). BAG 15.10.2013 – 1 ABR 25/12 is reported to apply this from a deployment of about four weeks — [gesetze.co](https://gesetze.co/urteile/1_ABR_25-12) (excerpt).
- LAG Mecklenburg-Vorpommern 4 TaBV 15/18: an agreed general internal posting "bedeutet für sich allein weder eine Festlegung auf den Kreis der Bewerber aus dem Betrieb" (i.e., no priority for insiders). — [anwalt24](https://www.anwalt24.de/browse/document/e7771bce-93b9-3da1-87a7-82030efd0bbe) (excerpt)
- LAG Düsseldorf (TaBV 46/18): the BR could refuse consent under §99(2) Nr. 5 where the employer ignored a §93 demand. The court rejected the argument that posting is unnecessary whenever the employer wants an external hire. — [LAG Düsseldorf PDF](https://www.lag-duesseldorf.nrw.de/beh_static/entscheidungen/entscheidungen/tabv/0046-18.pdf) (excerpt)

**Practice sources**

- "Eine interne und externe Stellenausschreibung können parallel durchgeführt werden." If both run, the requirements "müssen … identisch formuliert werden". A stricter internal profile is unequal treatment and regularly leads the BR to refuse consent to the external hire. The page also says §93 exempts managers ("Führungskräfte"). — [stellenmarkt.de personaltrends, 4 Nov 2018](https://personaltrends.stellenmarkt.de/interne-stellenausschreibung)
- German HR press (2026): a BR can require positions to be advertised internally first; a post may be filled internally while also advertised externally, so "Die Stelle muss deshalb nicht frei erfunden sein". — [ingenieur.de, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.ingenieur.de/karriere/arbeitsleben/ghost-jobs-wenn-die-traumstelle-nur-ein-koder-ist/)

### Inferences

- **Where the formality pattern comes from in Germany.** A BR that has invoked §93 forces a posting for every vacancy, including:
  - positions already earmarked for an internal candidate (the chief-physician case shows this);
  - planned conversions of temps (1 ABR 79/09);
  - trainee or junior take-overs (1 ABR 17/19).

  If the employer uses its public career site or job boards as the posting channel, these earmarked roles appear publicly. The vacancy is real, but it is not genuinely open to outsiders.
- **External ads are the employer's choice.** §93 requires only an internal posting. Any external ad is optional, so a pure "formality" external ad is a choice, not a legal must. Firms post externally partly to have comparison candidates, and partly because one ad serves both audiences. This is an inference: no data was found on how often external ads exist only for comparison.
- **The §99 paper trail.** Each hire requires presenting all applications to the BR. Fully fictitious "no vacancy at all" postings are therefore awkward in BR firms, because every hire generates documented applicant pools. The dominant BR-related ghost subtype is "real vacancy, outcome pre-decided", not "no vacancy".
- **Small BR firms.** §99, including the veto for a missing posting, applies only above 20 eligible employees. In BR establishments with 5–20 staff, §93 has no veto behind it, so the formality pressure is weaker there.
- **Executive roles.** Senior and executive roles are outside the BetrVG for leitende Angestellte (the practice source says managers are exempt; the statutory basis, §5(3) BetrVG, was not fetched). BR-driven formality postings should therefore be rare at the very top. Ghost mechanisms for executives (headhunter-led search, confidential replacement) are different.

### Gaps

- No data on what share of BRs have actually demanded §93 postings, or on works agreements prescribing "internal first for X weeks". No German statistic on the share of externally advertised roles ultimately filled internally.
- No BAG ruling found on whether the internal posting must come before external advertising in time. 1 ABR 16/21 covers only the timing relative to the decision and consent request. Parallel posting is lawful per practice sources.
- No data on how often internal candidates win after a parallel internal and external posting.

## 2. Wenske's Probezeit claim, notice periods (§622 BGB) and time-to-start

### Takeaway

- **Probezeit claim: unverified.** I found no evidence, survey or HR-practice source that German employers often keep the public ad online until a new hire completes the probation period (Probezeit). The only adjacent evidence is the BA's statement that some employers never deregister postings, and IAB evidence that fixed-term contracts are used as an "extended probation".
- **Notice periods: supported.** They are a documented cause of long time-to-start. IAB measured about 35 days between the hiring decision and the actual start for permanent hires (2018), and an "unplanned vacancy" of 27 days (2015, about 38 days for academics), explicitly citing notice periods.
- **Implication.** Far-future start dates (Eintrittsdatum) and a few extra weeks of visibility are normal for skilled roles and should not count as ghost evidence on their own.

### Cited Findings

- **§622 BGB:**
  - Basic notice: four weeks "zum Fünfzehnten oder zum Ende eines Kalendermonats".
  - Employer notice periods rise with tenure, from one month (2 years' service) to seven months (20 years).
  - During an agreed Probezeit of at most six months, either side may give two weeks' notice.
  - The employee's notice period may not be longer than the employer's.
  - Source: [§ 622 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__622.html)
- **IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019** (Gürtzgen, Kubis, Küfner; IAB-Stellenerhebung 2018):
  - Permanent hires: the whole process from start of search to actual start took 96 days (61 days to the hiring decision plus 35 days from decision to start).
  - Fixed-term hires: 82 days (55 plus 27).
  - Of 3.87 million new hires in 2018, 38% were fixed-term. 85% of those fixed-term hires served a longer-term need and "werden sie auch als verlängerte Probezeit genutzt".
  - Source: [IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2019/kb1719.pdf)
- **IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016** (Brenzel et al.; IAB-Stellenerhebung 2015):
  - Planned duration (start of search to desired start) averaged 58 days; actual duration 85 days. The 27-day "ungeplante Vakanzdauer" is explained by "die Kündigungsfrist in einem vorherigen Beschäftigungsverhältnis", relocation or employer delays.
  - Unskilled roles: planned 33 days, actual 53 days.
  - Vocational, Meister and Techniker roles: planned 63 days.
  - Academic roles: planned 69 days, actual 107 days, with about 38 days of unplanned vacancy.
  - Source: [IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2016/kb0416.pdf)
- **BA statistics:**
  - Every BA vacancy registration includes a desired filling date (gewünschter Besetzungstermin). Vacancy duration runs from that date, not from the posting date, to deregistration.
  - Means are distorted by late-deregistered postings, and the share of vacancies with more than 1,000 days' completed vacancy time has risen steadily since 2017.
  - Quote: "Dennoch gibt es Arbeitgeber die immer Personal suchen und aus diesem Grund ihre Stellen nicht abmelden bzw. durchgehend laufen lassen."
  - Source: [BA Hintergrundinfo Medianvakanzzeiten, Aug 2026](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Grundlagen/Methodik-Qualitaet/Methodenberichte/gemeldete-Arbeitsstellen/Generische-Publikationen/Hintergrundinfo-Medianvakanzzeiten-in-der-Statistik-der-gemeldeten-Arbeitsstellen.pdf)
- **Legal angle on keeping ads up after a decision:** in BAG 27.3.2025 – 8 AZR 123/24, the employer rebutted a presumption of disability discrimination by showing "dass das Auswahlverfahren bereits abgeschlossen war, bevor die Bewerbung der klagenden Partei bei ihm eingegangen ist" (Rn. 33). Applications arriving through a stale ad can be rejected, but the employer bears the burden of proof. — [BAG 8 AZR 123/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-123-24/)
- **US contrast only:** in a LiveCareer survey of 918 US HR professionals (25 Mar 2025), ghost-job ads stayed online under a week (15%), 1–4 weeks (43%), 1–3 months (37%) or indefinitely (5%). — [personalintern.de, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.personalintern.de/artikel/45-prozent-der-personaler-geben-zu-regelmaessig-ghost-jobs-auszuschreiben/)

### Inferences

- **The Probezeit-backup motive is plausible but unproven.** The two-week probation notice (§622(3)) gives employers a rationale to keep a backup pipeline for up to six months, and IAB shows German employers already hedge probation risk through fixed-term contracts. No data shows they do it by leaving ads online.
- **A testable signature in BA data.** If the claim were common, BA-registered filled vacancies would show a cluster of deregistrations about 6 months after the desired start date. Nobody seems to have tested this (gap).
- **Notice periods explain far-future start dates and some extra ad visibility, not months-long open ads by themselves.**
  - The decision-to-start gap (about 35 days on average; longer for experienced staff with contractual 3-month notice) happens after the ad has done its job.
  - A genuine ad needs to be live only during the application window. IAB/Gürtzgen-Moczall put the average application window at about 18 days and the selection phase at about 45 days for pool-based search (Section 6).
  - So a role with a start date 3–6 months away is consistent with genuine hiring. An ad that remains live long after its own stated start date is the stronger staleness cue.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Notice periods push actual start 1–3+ months after the decision | Start-date distance; posting age | Far-future Eintrittsdatum: neutral or slightly lowers ghost probability for Fachkraft, Spezialist and Experte roles | Parse "Eintritt zum 01.04.2027", "zum nächstmöglichen Zeitpunkt", "ab sofort" | High (statute and IAB) |
| Desired start date already passed while the ad stays live | Days since desired start (the BA's vacancy-duration definition) | Raises ghost or stale probability once it exceeds the occupation's median vacancy duration (Section 5) | BA Jobbörse desired start date or parsed "ab sofort" combined with last-seen date | High for the definition; Medium for the threshold |
| Never-deregistered "always hiring" postings | Posting age; reposting | Raise (evergreen subtype) | Same ad text online for longer than about 2x the occupation median; repeated identical reposts | High (BA states it exists); prevalence unknown |
| Alleged Probezeit-backup ads | Posting age after a hire | Indistinguishable from stale ads; do not model separately | n/a | Low (unverified) |

### Gaps

- No German survey or HR-practice source on keeping ads online after a hire or through probation. Wenske's own source is unknown because the article was not found.
- No representative German distribution of contractual notice periods. The common "3 months to quarter-end" (3 Monate zum Quartalsende) for experienced staff could not be quantified. Statute and IAB averages are the only hard anchors.
- No recent (2023–2026) IAB decomposition of decision-to-start times by qualification. The latest found is for 2018 (overall) and 2015 (by qualification).

## 3. Public sector: duty to advertise (Ausschreibungspflicht), best-candidate selection, Konkurrentenstreit, §§164/165 SGB IX and the BA Jobbörse

### Takeaway

- **Duty to advertise.** Federal civil-service posts must be advertised, and publicly when hiring from outside (§8 BBG). Professorships must be advertised publicly and as a rule internationally (§45 HRG). In the federal service, any decision not to advertise needs the staff council's (Personalrat) co-determination (§78 BPersVG).
- **Selection is litigable.** Art. 33(2) GG makes every selection subject to court review by rejected candidates (Konkurrentenstreit). Aborting a procedure needs a documented objective reason.
- **Result: genuine-looking processes.** Public postings exist even where a favoured internal candidate is likely, but the process must be formally genuine. The subtype is "pre-decided but contestable", not "no vacancy".
- **BA notification.** §165 SGB IX obliges public employers to report vacancies to the Agentur für Arbeit, which counts as consent to publish them. The BAG now requires all employers to issue a formal placement order (Vermittlungsauftrag) to the BA under §164 SGB IX. Many BA Jobbörse listings are therefore compliance registrations attached to real procedures.

### Cited Findings

- §8 BBG: "Zu besetzende Stellen sind auszuschreiben. Bei der Einstellung von Bewerberinnen und Bewerbern muss die Ausschreibung öffentlich sein." The federal government may set exceptions by ordinance. — [§ 8 BBG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbg_2009/__8.html)
- §78 Abs. 1 BPersVG: the Personalrat co-determines Nr. 1 "Einstellung" and Nr. 12 "Absehen von der Ausschreibung von Dienstposten, die besetzt werden sollen". — [§ 78 BPersVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bpersvg_2021/__78.html)
- §45 HRG: professorships "sind öffentlich und im Regelfall international auszuschreiben". Land law may provide exceptions, "insbesondere wenn eine Juniorprofessorin oder ein Juniorprofessor auf eine Professur berufen werden soll". — [§ 45 HRG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hrg/__45.html)
- **Art. 33(2) GG and Konkurrentenstreit:**
  - Art. 33(2) GG gives every German equal access to public office by aptitude, qualifications and professional performance (Eignung, Befähigung, fachliche Leistung). With Art. 19(4), a rejected candidate can have the selection judicially reviewed.
  - The employer has wide discretion to abort a procedure, but needs an objective reason, and may not use the abort to "gezielt und willkürlich auszuschalten" a candidate.
  - A decision not to fill the post at all is a matter of organisational power and is not limited by applicants' rights.
  - Source: [rechtslupe](https://www.rechtslupe.de/verwaltungsrecht/beamtenrecht/rechtsschutz-beim-konkurrentenstreit-und-der-abbruch-des-auswahlverfahrens-336475) (excerpt)
- VGH München 11.11.2024 – 3 CE 24.1481: applicants must learn of an abort in time and the main reason must be documented in writing; after a court has objected, the employer must at least plausibly explain why the procedure cannot continue. A one-month period applies for urgent applications against an abort. BVerfG 2 BvR 1181/11 found a violation where the abort reason was insufficiently documented. — [Landesanwaltschaft Bayern, 26 Nov 2024](https://www.anwaltschaft.bayern.de/media/themenbereiche/oeffentlicher_dienst_und_disziplinarrecht/2024-11-26_beamtenrecht.pdf) (excerpt)
- HR expert Matthias Mölleney (HWZ Zürich, speaking to RND): large companies and the public sector may be legally required to advertise even when an internal candidate is already chosen. — [Computerwoche, 23 Sep 2024](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/3535407/immer-mehr-bewerber-sehen-gespenster.html). Counterpoint: public-sector rules on transparent, merit-based selection "do not create a general duty to publicly advertise a position whose outcome is already settled" ("Daraus folgt jedoch keine allgemeine Pflicht, eine Position öffentlich auszuschreiben"). — [ingenieur.de, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.ingenieur.de/karriere/arbeitsleben/ghost-jobs-wenn-die-traumstelle-nur-ein-koder-ist/)
- **§165 SGB IX:** public employers report vacancies, soon-to-be-vacant posts and new posts to the Agenturen für Arbeit early, after an unsuccessful check for internal filling. The report counts as consent to publish the vacancy. Severely disabled applicants must be invited to interview unless "die fachliche Eignung offensichtlich fehlt". — [§ 165 SGB IX](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_9_2018/__165.html)
- **§164 Abs. 1 SGB IX** applies to all employers. They must check whether vacancies can be filled with severely disabled people, and "nehmen frühzeitig Verbindung mit der Agentur für Arbeit auf". The BA or an integration service then proposes candidates, and the disability representatives must be informed. — [§ 164 SGB IX](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_9_2018/__164.html)
- **BAG 27.3.2025 – 8 AZR 123/24** (private employer):
  - Leitsatz: the §164(1) S. 2 duty requires issuing a placement order (Vermittlungsauftrag). Merely posting in the BA online job board "ist daher nicht ausreichend" (Rn. 24).
  - A breach can found a presumption of disability discrimination under §22 AGG (Rn. 22).
  - The rule from §165 (public employers) "ist auf § 164 Abs. 1 Satz 2 SGB IX zu übertragen" (Rn. 26).
  - Source: [BAG 8 AZR 123/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-123-24/)
- **Context figures:** public administration has a Personalrat in 65% of establishments, covering 94% of employees, and collective-agreement coverage (sectoral or company) for 98% of employees. — [WSI-Mitteilungen 4/2025](https://www.boeckler.de/data/wsimit_2025_04_hohendanner-1.pdf). Public-administration ads raised their salary-disclosure share by 5.4 points in Index's H1 2024–2026 comparison; the exact base year was not stated in the retrieved summary. — [Personalwirtschaft/Index Research, Jul 2026](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=206404)

### Inferences

- **Two signals point opposite ways for public ads.**
  - The duty to advertise means many public postings are formality-adjacent (a strong internal or "house" candidate).
  - Every selection is documented and contestable, and abort decisions must be documented, so outright fake public postings are legally risky.
  - Score public employers with a higher prior for "outcome pre-decided" and a lower prior for "no vacancy / evergreen".
- **Reporting delay to the BA.** §165 says public employers report to the BA after the internal check fails. BA Jobbörse copies of public jobs can appear later than the employer's own portal (Interamt, service.bund.de, Land portals). They should be deduplicated, not treated as reposts.
- **Compliance registrations at the BA.** After BAG 8 AZR 123/24, risk-averse employers will issue a placement order for most vacancies. "Listed with the BA" therefore says little about active intent beyond a formal process, and such listings may outlive the selection because deregistration is a separate step (see the BA statement in Section 2).
- **Professorships.** Appointment procedures (Berufungsverfahren) are long. Durations were not found, so a separate baseline is needed. Tenure-track and junior-professor conversions may happen without an ad, so the posted universe is skewed toward genuinely open calls.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Staffing agencies: temp agencies (Arbeitnehmerüberlassung), private recruiters (Personalvermittler), pool ads (Vorratsanzeigen) and duplicates

### Takeaway

- **Official confirmation of pool postings.** The Bundesagentur für Arbeit itself states that temp-agency vacancy registrations are oriented to expected future orders and "teilweise zunächst der Portfoliobildung dienen" (partly serve portfolio-building at first). They are filled only once a client order arrives. This is the clearest official German confirmation of speculative "pool" postings.
- **Volume.** About one fifth of BA-registered vacancies recently came from temp agencies, against 1.8% of employment. Agency ads are a large, structurally different segment that needs its own baseline.
- **Online ads.** No reliable figure for the agency share of online (non-BA) ads was found.

### Cited Findings

- **BA Blickpunkt Arbeitsmarkt – Entwicklungen in der Zeitarbeit (January 2026).** Source for all items in this bullet: [BA Zeitarbeit report, Jan 2026](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Statistiken/Themen-im-Fokus/Zeitarbeit/generische-Publikation/Arbeitsmarkt-Deutschland-Zeitarbeit-Aktuelle-Entwicklung.pdf)
  - "So richten sich die Stellenmeldungen aus dieser Branche sehr stark an erwarteten Aufträgen für die Zukunft aus. Dies bedeutet, dass die gemeldeten Stellenbedarfe teilweise zunächst der Portfoliobildung dienen und diese Stellen gegebenenfalls erst zu einem späteren Zeitpunkt besetzt werden, wenn der Auftrag tatsächlich erteilt wurde."
  - Share of all BA-registered vacancies coming from temp agencies:
    - 2005–2011: between a fifth and well over a third;
    - following years: about one third;
    - 2021: 27%;
    - most recently: "etwa ein Fünftel".
  - The stock of temp-agency vacancies was 131,000 in December 2025, down 9% year on year and the lowest December value since 2010.
  - Monthly new registrations have been about 23,000 since 2024, a historic low.
  - The statistics cannot distinguish vacancies for temp workers from vacancies for the agencies' own staff.
  - 622,000 temp workers in June 2025 made up 1.8% of employment; more than half work in helper-level (Helfer) jobs.
- **BA totals for 2025:** average vacancy stock 632,000 (62,000 fewer than 2024); 1,461,000 new registrations, the lowest in 25 years. — [BA Jahresrückblick 2025, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/presse/2026-02-jahresrueckblick-2025)
- **Salary disclosure:** "Sonstige wirtschaftliche Dienstleistungen", which Index says consists mostly of staffing agencies, had the highest salary-disclosure share at about 40% in H1 2026, against 24.1% overall. — [Personalwirtschaft/Index Research, Jul 2026](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=206404)
- **Duplicates:** Greenhouse co-founder Jon Stross cites agencies leaving duplicate ads online as one cause of ghost jobs. — [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/karriere/human-resources/darum-sind-ghost-jobs-fur-unternehmen-riskant)
- **Works-council link:** positions to be filled with temps fall under §93 internal posting at the client firm (BAG 1 ABR 79/09). — [BAG](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/?p=1146206) (excerpt)

### Inferences

- **Treat temp-agency ads as their own class.** Evergreen and portfolio postings are part of the business model, and the BA says so. Give them a separate baseline and an "agency pool" label, rather than scoring them against direct-employer norms.
- **Rough stock-flow check (my calculation, Little's law).**
  - All BA vacancies: 632,000 average stock ÷ 1,461,000 annual inflow ≈ 0.43 years ≈ 158 days average time registered.
  - Temp agencies: 131,000 December stock ÷ about 23,000 monthly inflow ≈ 5.7 months ≈ 170 days.
  - Agency vacancies therefore stay registered slightly longer on average. This is consistent with portfolio-building. The comparison is rough: it mixes December and annual averages, and agency stock includes internal-staff vacancies.
- **Duplicates and anonymous client ads.** Duplicate postings across several agencies for one client job ("für unseren Kunden", no employer name, the same requirements and location) inflate apparent demand. They should be clustered, and only the cluster counted for demand. No German data was found on how often this happens.
- **Missing salary is more informative for agency ads.** Agencies disclose pay far more often (about 40%), partly because pay follows agency collective agreements. Missing pay in an agency ad is therefore somewhat more unusual than in a direct-employer ad.

| Mechanism | Feature(s) | Effect | How to detect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portfolio and pool registrations by temp agencies | Posting source; speculative-pool subtype | Raise "speculative / pool" probability; use a separate baseline | Employer in NACE 78.2 or 78.3; name contains "Personal(dienst)leistung", "Personalservice", "Zeitarbeit"; text contains "Arbeitnehmerüberlassung", "für unseren Kunden", "Einsatz bei", "Übernahmeoption", "Tarifvertrag GVP/BAP/iGZ" | High (BA official statement) |
| Agency over-representation (about 20% of BA vacancies vs 1.8% of employment) | Prior by source | Baseline adjustment | Share of the corpus from agencies | High |
| Duplicate and anonymised client ads | Duplicate clusters | Count once; anonymous ad plus multiple agencies plus long life raises pool probability | Near-duplicate text across poster names; missing end-client name | Low–Medium |
| Agencies disclose salary more often | Missing-salary feature | Missing salary in an agency ad: mildly raise | Agency flag × salary absent | Medium |

### Gaps

- No figure found for the share of online ads (job boards, company sites) from staffing agencies or private recruiters (Personalvermittler) in Germany. Index or Textkernel data would be needed, and the search budget ran out.
- No German data on the frequency of pool ads (Vorratsanzeigen), anonymised client ads, or multi-agency duplicates.
- No documentation retrieved on BA Jobbörse rules for agency or anonymous listings. The idea that the Jobbörse separately flags Zeitarbeit and private recruiter offers is unverified here; check against the data-source researcher's notes.

## 5. Skills shortages and posting-duration baselines (BA Engpassanalyse, completed vacancy durations, IAB recruitment durations)

### Takeaway

- **Use occupation medians as the baseline.** Long vacancy durations are normal in German shortage occupations (Engpassberufe), especially construction and skilled trades (SHK, Trockenbau), care and medicine. Baselines should use BA occupation-level median vacancy durations, measured from the desired start date.
- **Read the BA's switch to medians as a warning.** The BA moved to medians because means are inflated by never-deregistered postings, which is itself evidence that stale postings exist.
- **Qualification matters.** IAB data show high-qualification roles take about twice as long as unskilled ones.

### Cited Findings

- **BA Engpassanalyse 2025** (source for this bullet: [BA Fachkräfteengpassanalyse 2025](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Statistiken/Themen-im-Fokus/Fachkraeftebedarf/Fachkraefteengpassanalyse/Fachkraefteengpassanalyse.html)):
  - 157 shortage occupations in 2025 (163 in 2024, 183 in 2023), with 161 further occupation types under observation.
  - Main shortage groups: care, medical, and construction and skilled-trade occupations; also cooks, professional drivers and early-childhood educators.
  - No longer in shortage: spanende Metallbearbeitung, IT-Vertrieb and Softwareentwicklung. New shortages: Vermessungstechnik and Sozialarbeit/Sozialpädagogik.
  - Most shortage occupations are at Fachkraft level, about one third at Spezialist level and one sixth at Experte level.
  - "Derzeit gibt es keine Belege für einen allgemeinen Arbeitskräftemangel."
- **Method:** the vacancy-duration indicator is one of six indicators. It measures the time from the desired filling date to deregistration of the vacancy with the BA's employer service, using the 2025 median. Occupations scoring 2.0 or more count as shortage occupations; below 1.5 as no shortage. — [BA Engpassanalyse interactive](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Navigation/Statistiken/Interaktive-Statistiken/Fachkraeftebedarf/Engpassanalyse-Nav.html) (excerpt)
- **BA method note (August 2026):** source for this bullet: [BA Hintergrundinfo, Aug 2026](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Grundlagen/Methodik-Qualitaet/Methodenberichte/gemeldete-Arbeitsstellen/Generische-Publikationen/Hintergrundinfo-Medianvakanzzeiten-in-der-Statistik-der-gemeldeten-Arbeitsstellen.pdf)
  - Example: "Im Jahr 2025 konnten in Deutschland Arbeitsstellen im Hochbau durchschnittlich 277 Tage nicht besetzt werden" (mean).
  - Hamburg: the mean completed vacancy duration jumped from 115 days (October 2023) to 229 days (November 2023), while the median rose only from 73 to 85 days.
  - The share of vacancies with more than 1,000 days has risen since 2017. Many such cases are implausible, though some employers do search very long without success.
  - The median becomes the standard measure; most BA products switch by about the end of October 2026.
- **Longest vacancy durations by occupation** (BA data via Statista, 24 Feb 2026; the page's reference period is inconsistent, either Feb 2025–Jan 2026 or Sep 2025–Aug 2026, and probably means):

  | Occupation | Days |

  | Berg-/Tagebau- und Sprengtechnik | 299 |
  | Naturstein-, Mineral- und Baustoffherstellung | 298 |

- **Regional anchor:** in Hessen the average completed vacancy duration was 158 days in September 2025 (142 in August, 161 in July, 163 in April 2025). — [IWAK Regionaldatenreport, Wiesbaden](https://www.wiesbaden.de/medien/downloads/IWAK_Regionaldatenreport_55.pdf) (excerpt; the exact issue was not verified). National average reported at 138 days for 2023, citing the BA. — [StepStone](https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/hr-wissen/recruiting/vakanzkosten) (excerpt)

  - 2015: 85 days from start of search to start of work on average; unskilled 53 days, academics 107 days.
  - East used to take longer than West, but by 2015 both took equally long (West rose from 74 days in 2014 to 85 in 2015).
  - Source: [IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2016/kb0416.pdf)
  - 2018: 96 days for permanent and 82 days for fixed-term hires. — [IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2019/kb1719.pdf)

### Inferences

- **Recommended baseline: ghost-likelihood from posting age should be relative, not absolute.**
  - Compute days since the desired start date, or since first seen if no start date is given.
  - Normalise by the median vacancy duration for the occupation's 5-digit KldB 2010 code (Berufsgattung). Adjust further by qualification level (Helfer, Fachkraft, Spezialist, Experte) and region.
  - A 200-day-old Trockenbau or SHK ad is near the norm. A 200-day-old ad for a non-shortage office role is not.
- **IT example.** Softwareentwicklung lost shortage status in 2025, so long-open developer ads are less justified by shortage than they were in 2022–2023. This is relevant to the "tech evergreen" pattern in Section 10.
- **Use medians, not means.** BA means are contaminated by exactly the never-deregistered postings a ghost model is trying to find. Using BA means as the "normal" baseline would understate staleness.
- **Rough overall anchor.** Little's law on 2025 BA totals gives about 158 days average registration time (Section 4), consistent with Hessen's 142–163-day monthly means.
- **Transferability caveat.** BA-registered vacancies are a subset of all ads: Index counted 5.87 million advertised positions in H1 2026 across print, online, the BA portal and company sites, against 1.46 million BA registrations in all of 2025. BA duration baselines may not transfer one-to-one to job-board ads. This is an inference; the definitions differ.

| Mechanism | Feature(s) | Effect | How to detect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shortage occupations with structurally long vacancies | Posting-age threshold | Baseline adjustment: raise the age threshold, e.g., multiples of the occupation median | Map the title to KldB 2010; join BA Engpass status and median vacancy duration | High |
| Qualification gradient (academic roles take about 2x unskilled) | Posting-age threshold | Baseline adjustment | Anforderungsniveau (KldB 5th digit) or requirement parsing | Medium–High (IAB, older data) |
| BA means inflated by never-deregistered ads | Training labels and baselines | Use medians; treat extreme-tail ages as a candidate ghost signal | BA median tables (switch due by end of October 2026) | High |
| Occupations that left shortage status (software, IT sales, machining) | Posting-age threshold | Lower tolerance than in 2022–2023 | Same mapping | High (status) / Medium (implication) |

### Gaps

- National median vacancy duration across all occupations for 2025 or 2026, and the full occupation table, were not retrieved. They are in the BA Engpassanalyse data annex and Excel tables.
- No recent (2023–2026) IAB-Stellenerhebung figures on recruitment duration or the share of abandoned searches. Newer IAB Kurzberichte were not found before the search budget ran out.
- The regional figures (Hessen) and the 2023 national average are excerpt-level only.

## 6. German recruiting studies: talent pools, evergreen ads, time-to-hire, internal hires and employers ghosting applicants

### Takeaway

- **Pool-based search is normal.** German employers predominantly collect a pool of applications before choosing; about 75% of successful hires use this approach. Application windows average about 18 days.
- **Many hires bypass ads.** Personal contacts fill about one in three positions.
- **Applicant ghosting is widespread.** 63.5% of employees in Germany who applied in the past 12 months got no response at least once (2026). Applicant-reported non-response is therefore not evidence of a ghost posting.
- **Studies not retrieved.** The Bamberg CHRIS "Recruiting Trends" and "Bewerbungspraxis" series, and the StepStone, Softgarden, Personio and XING studies, could not be retrieved (search budget exhausted; the Bamberg URLs tried returned 404).

### Cited Findings

- **Gürtzgen & Moczall (2019), IAB-Discussion Paper 18/2019** (abstract reproduced in IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019):
  - Non-sequential search (collect a pool, then pick the best) accounts for "etwa 75 Prozent aller erfolgreichen Einstellungen".
  - It is used disproportionately by larger establishments and for highly qualified positions.
  - Non-sequential search: application period about 18 days, selection period 45 days.
  - Sequential search: first application after about 1 day, then a combined application and selection phase of 57 days.
  - Source: [IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2019/kb1719.pdf)
- **IAB-Stellenerhebung 2015:** "fast jede dritte Stelle" was filled through personal contacts. BA placement services and newspaper ads each matched about one in seven hires. Academics were most often hired through internet job boards. — [IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2016/kb0416.pdf)
- **Internal postings (IAB, 2010):**
  - Used as a search channel for about 22.6% of new hires (West 23.8%, East 16.5%).
  - As the successful channel, they accounted for only 3.2% of new hires (West 3.4%, East 2.3%).
  - An older wave (Q4 2006) put both internal postings and internal internships at about 2% of new hires each.
  - Sources: [IAB Kurzgrafik](https://doku.iab.de/kurzgraf/2011/kbfolien26112.pdf); [IAB Kurzgrafik](https://doku.iab.de/kurzgraf/2011/kbfolien26113.pdf); [weka](https://www.weka.de/betriebsrat-personalrat/neueinstellungen-beruhen-ueberwiegend-auf-persoenlichen-kontakten/) (all excerpt; the IAB "new hires" concept covers hires into the establishment, so it does not measure internal transfers)
- **Appinio survey for Indeed** (1,000 employees in Germany, 9–11 March 2026):
  - 63.5% received no response from an employer at least once in the past 12 months after applying; 23% experienced this regularly.
  - 78.3% of those affected said ghosting had increased.
  - The article stresses this does not show the applications were for ghost jobs.
  - Indeed Germany's managing director, Frank Hensgens, is quoted.
  - Source: [ingenieur.de, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.ingenieur.de/karriere/arbeitsleben/ghost-jobs-wenn-die-traumstelle-nur-ein-koder-ist/)
- **Greenhouse Candidate Experience Report** (2,900 employees in DACH, the US, the UK and Ireland; fieldwork date not stated):
  - 65% in DACH said employers stopped responding during an ongoing process.
  - 37% expect first feedback within two weeks and another 25% within a month.
  - DACH ghosting was unchanged while the US rate fell 22% year on year.
  - Source: [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/karriere/human-resources/darum-sind-ghost-jobs-fur-unternehmen-riskant)
- **US contrast:** in the LiveCareer survey (918 US HR professionals, March 2025), 45% "regularly" and 48% "occasionally" post roles they do not intend to fill promptly; 69% often close postings without further response to applicants. Reasons for ghosting candidates: lost interest (37%), position on hold (28%), changed priorities (27%). — [personalintern.de](https://www.personalintern.de/artikel/45-prozent-der-personaler-geben-zu-regelmaessig-ghost-jobs-auszuschreiben/)

### Inferences

- **Pool collection is normal, open-ended visibility is not.** A short "collect-then-select" phase of about 3 weeks is standard German practice and is not ghosting. Postings live far beyond application window plus selection (roughly 2–3 months), with no change and no deadline, fall outside this norm.
- **Do not use applicant non-response as a ghost label.** Non-response is endemic (about 64% of applicants affected), so applicant-reported "no answer" is a poor training label without other evidence.
- **Internal hiring is under-measured.** The IAB internal-posting figures measure hires into the establishment, not internal moves, so the true share of vacancies filled internally is unknown in public statistics.

| Mechanism | Feature(s) | Effect | How to detect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pool-based search with about 18-day application windows | Posting-age norm | Baseline: normal visible life about 3–8 weeks for direct-employer ads, before occupation adjustment | First-seen / last-seen dates; "Bewerbungsfrist" | Medium (IAB 2018 data) |
| Widespread employer non-response | Label quality | Do not treat applicant non-response as ground truth | n/a | Medium (one 2026 German survey) |
| One third of hires through personal contacts | Coverage | Many filled roles never appear as ads; does not change the score | n/a | Medium (2015 data) |

### Gaps

- Not retrieved: the Uni Bamberg CHRIS / Monster / Indeed "Recruiting Trends" and "Bewerbungspraxis" studies (talent pools, active sourcing, share of internal hires, response times); StepStone, Softgarden, Personio and XING surveys on time-to-hire, evergreen ads and ghosting. A follow-up search pass is needed.
- No German data on the prevalence of evergreen or talent-pool postings among direct employers.

## 7. EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970): German transposition status and salary disclosure in ads

### Takeaway

- **Status: not transposed.** As of early October 2026, Germany has missed the 7 June 2026 deadline. No ministry draft bill (Referentenentwurf) has been published. The 2017 Entgelttransparenzgesetz still applies unchanged and contains no rules on pay information in job ads. Stating a salary range in an ad remains voluntary.
- **Expected timing.** The most specific reported timeline (law in 2027, duties from June 2028) is not officially confirmed.
- **Disclosure rates.** Only about 12–16% of German ads state a salary by Indeed's measure (up to about 24% including collective-agreement references, or by Index Research's broader count).
- **Implication.** Missing salary is the German norm and a weak ghost signal today. It may only partly change after transposition, because Art. 5 also allows disclosure "before the interview".

### Cited Findings

  - The directive's four instruments include "Gehaltsangaben vor und bei Einstellung (Art. 5 f.)". — [Bundestag WD EU 6 - 3000 - 076/26, completed 8 Jun 2026](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1184992/EU-6-076-26.pdf)

    - public administration: up 5.4 points.
  - By region: North Rhine-Westphalia 27.6% (highest), Saarland 18.8% (lowest).
- **Conflicting figures:** 12% (Indeed, May 2026), 15.8%/23.7% (Indeed, end 2024), 16% (Haufe, Apr 2026) and 24.1% (Index, H1 2026). The differences reflect platform coverage (Indeed-indexed postings vs. Index's multi-media position count) and whether collective-agreement references count. The Indeed May 2026 claim that benchmark references do not change results conflicts with its own March 2025 figure (15.8% vs 23.7% with references), probably a methodology change.

### Inferences

- **Now.** Missing salary is the default: about 76–88% of German ads omit it. Use "salary missing relative to the norm for its segment" instead of a raw flag. Missing salary is informative mainly where disclosure is common (cleaning, transport, hourly roles, staffing agencies at about 40%, public employers quoting TVöD/TV-L/A grades). It carries almost no information for IT, engineering, legal and senior roles (≤12% disclosure for senior hierarchy levels).
- **Collective-agreement references count as pay information.** "TVöD E 13", "TV-L E 11" or "A 13" should be parsed as pay information; otherwise public and tariff-bound employers will be mis-scored.
- **After transposition (realistically 2027 at the earliest; not before a German statute exists):**
  - If Germany requires pay in the ad itself, missing salary becomes a compliance-deviation signal and gains weight.
  - If it lets employers provide pay "before the interview" (allowed by Art. 5), missing salary in the ad stays lawful and only somewhat more informative.
  - The model should key this feature to an effective-date switch and to the final German wording. Do not assume the June 2026 EU deadline changed employer behaviour.
- **Direct effect.** Possible direct effect of Art. 5 against public employers after the deadline is a legal question (vertical direct effect of precise directive provisions). No source here confirms that German public employers act on it. Treat it as speculative.

| Mechanism | Feature(s) | Effect | How to detect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low German disclosure norm (12–24%) | Missing salary | Weak positive at most; normalise by occupation, seniority, source and region | Salary regex plus collective-agreement and civil-service grade parser | Medium–High (two independent datasets, levels differ) |
| Segments with high disclosure (cleaning, transport, agencies, public sector) | Missing salary | Absence more informative: raise modestly | Segment × salary absent | Medium |
| Senior and IT ads rarely disclose | Missing salary | Neutral | Seniority and occupation classifier | Medium |
| Future Art. 5 transposition | Missing salary (time-varying) | Increase the weight only after German law takes effect, and only if ad-level disclosure is mandatory | Date of posting vs. effective date | High (status) / unknown (content) |

### Gaps

- The content of any German draft (ad-level vs. pre-interview disclosure, size thresholds, sanctions) is unknown; no Referentenentwurf existed as of 2 October 2026. I could not confirm whether anything was published between 2 and 24 October 2026.
- The commission's final-report recommendations on Art. 5 specifically were not retrieved.
- StepStone's own disclosure figures could not be fetched (page timed out twice).

## 8. Legality: UWG, AGG, culpa in contrahendo; political initiatives; residence-permit and labour-market-test rules

### Takeaway

- **Not illegal as such.** I found no German statute, court decision, regulator action or political initiative that prohibits or sanctions posting a job without intent to hire. Ghost postings are legally low-risk in Germany.
- **Where the legal pressure actually acts:**
  - AGG discrimination liability attaches to real selection processes. Courts police abuse by sham applicants ("AGG-Hopper"), not sham postings.
  - §164 SGB IX plus BAG 8 AZR 123/24 push employers to register vacancies formally with the BA.
- **UWG applies to recruitment conduct only in principle.** No ghost-ad case was found.
- **Immigration rules do not generate public postings.** Germany has no US-PERM-style advertising requirement. The labour-market test (Vorrangprüfung) is off by default for skilled workers and Blue Card holders and is done internally by the BA where it applies (e.g., the Westbalkan rule).

### Cited Findings

- **AGG and sham applicants:**
  - BAG 19.9.2024 – 8 AZR 21/24: compensation under §15(2) AGG is barred by abuse of rights (§242 BGB) if the person applied not to get the job but only to obtain applicant status in order to claim compensation (Rn. 24). This requires objective indications plus an intent to gain an unjustified advantage (Rn. 26); the employer bears the burden (Rn. 27).
  - Many applications alone are not enough (Rn. 49). A "systematisches und zielgerichtetes Vorgehen" is required (Rn. 50). Here: nationwide applications for secretary posts followed by compensation suits, treated as a business model.
  - Source: [BAG 8 AZR 21/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-21-24/)
  - Basis: CJEU 28.7.2016 – C-423/15 (Kratzer) — sham applications aimed only at compensation fall outside the anti-discrimination directives. — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/de/insights/schadenersatzanspruche-nach-agg-auch-fur-scheinbewerber) (excerpt)
  - Lower courts continue the line, e.g., ArbG Bamberg 29.9.2025 – 3 Ca 662/24 (abuse finding can rely on an abuse finding in another case). — [gesetze-bayern.de](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Pdf/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2025-N-48662?all=True) (excerpt)
- **AGG and BA registration:** BAG 27.3.2025 – 8 AZR 123/24. Failing to issue a placement order to the BA (§164(1) S. 2 SGB IX; a Jobbörse posting alone is not enough) can found a presumption of disability discrimination. The presumption can be rebutted if selection was completed before the application arrived. — [BAG 8 AZR 123/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-123-24/)
- **UWG:** BGH 2002 (I ZR 115/00) held that job ads which also work as image advertising for the firm must comply with fair-competition rules (the ads at issue were not unfair). BGH 2004 (I ZR 221/01) applied the UWG to headhunter poaching calls. No BGH or OLG ruling was found treating a misleading or ghost job ad as unfair under §5 UWG. — [lexetius I ZR 115/00](https://lexetius.com/2002,3282) (excerpt); [lexetius I ZR 221/01](https://lexetius.com/2004,983) (excerpt)
- **General practice view:**
  - "Einen direkten Anspruch auf Bearbeitung oder Beantwortung von Bewerbungen gibt es in Deutschland nicht."
  - Ghost jobs could breach good faith (§242 BGB); damages are conceivable only if deliberate deception is proven.
  - Applicant data must be deleted or anonymised after the process (GDPR / DSGVO).
  - Source: [unternehmer.de, 25 May 2026](https://unternehmer.de/wirtschaft/625515-ghost-jobs-jede-dritte-stellenanzeige-betroffen)
  - Media consensus: ghost jobs "sind in den meisten Ländern nicht illegal". — [leinetal24, 10 Jul 2026](https://www.leinetal24.de/verbraucher/ghost-jobs-so-erkennen-sie-fake-stellenanzeigen-im-netz-zr-94391464.html)
- **Political initiatives:** a search for German bans or initiatives on ghost or fake job ads (2026) surfaced only foreign items (a New York bill requiring hiring timelines; a Texas Attorney General probe of LinkedIn) and no German initiative. — [t3n on the New York bill](https://t3n.de/news/schluss-mit-falschen-stellenanzeigen-dieser-us-bundesstaat-will-ghost-jobs-verbieten-1747564/); [Staffing Industry Analysts on Texas](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/linkedin-investigated-over-listings-for-ghost-jobs)
- **Residence-permit rules:**
  - §39(2) AufenthG: BA consent for skilled workers (§§18a, 18b) and the EU Blue Card (§18g) is given "ohne Vorrangprüfung … es sei denn" the Employment Ordinance (BeschV) provides otherwise. Conditions are equal working conditions and a domestic employment relationship. The employer's only duty is to provide information on request (§39(4)). The text contains no duty to advertise or register the vacancy. — [§ 39 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__39.html)
  - §26(2) BeschV (Westbalkan rule): consent "mit Vorrangprüfung zur Ausübung jeder Beschäftigung", capped at up to 50,000 approvals per calendar year; first approvals require the visa application at a German mission abroad. No advertising or registration duty is stated in the text. — [§ 26 BeschV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/beschv_2013/__26.html)

### Inferences

- **No legal deterrent.** No statute, enforcement body or case law targets ghost postings. The only costs are reputational and the AGG exposure of running real processes. German employers can leave ads up or post speculatively with little legal risk, consistent with the BA's observation that some ads are never deregistered.
- **AGG cuts both ways.**
  - Employers worried about AGG claims will prefer formal, documented processes: posting, BA registration, inviting severely disabled applicants. This raises the number of postings attached to processes whose outcome may already be clear.
  - The AGG-Hopper case law means sham-application risk is managed by the courts, not by avoiding postings.
- **Immigration.** Do not model immigration rules as a source of fake public postings in Germany. Where a labour-market test applies (Westbalkan), it is done by the BA, not through public ads. Whether the BA in practice asks employers to register the vacancy in those cases was not verified.

| Mechanism | Feature(s) | Effect | How to detect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No legal prohibition or enforcement | Prior ghost rate | No downward legal adjustment, unlike jurisdictions with ghost-ad laws | n/a | Medium (absence of evidence) |
| AGG and SGB IX compliance formalities | BA listing; "Schwerbehinderte werden bevorzugt" boilerplate | Neutral for genuineness; marks a formal process (slightly lowers "no vacancy") | Boilerplate detection | High (law) / Medium (inference) |
| Immigration Vorrangprüfung | n/a | Not a source of postings | n/a | Medium (statute text; BA practice unverified) |

### Gaps

- Culpa in contrahendo case law (§311(2) BGB liability for breaking off hiring negotiations, interview-cost reimbursement) was not retrieved. It is standard doctrine but has no source here.
- No German case on AGG claims by applicants to positions that were never meant to be filled, or on UWG or consumer-protection action against ghost ads. The search budget ran out before targeted searches; absence should be confirmed in a follow-up.
- BA Jobbörse terms of use on genuine vacancies and pool ads were not retrieved.

## 9. German media coverage, 2025–2026 (Ghost Jobs, Geisterjobs, Phantomstellen, Scheinausschreibung)

### Takeaway

- **Coverage is regular but borrowed.** German media covered ghost jobs regularly from 2024 to 2026 (Computerwoche, OnlineMarketing.de, unternehmer.de, ingenieur.de, Ippen regional titles, personalintern.de, t3n). Every headline prevalence figure ("knapp 30%", "jede dritte", "bis zu 40%", "45% der Personaler") traces to US or UK data, or to no source.
- **The only German-sample numbers** concern applicant ghosting (Appinio/Indeed, March 2026; Greenhouse DACH). No German measurement of ghost-posting prevalence exists in the coverage found.

### Cited Findings

- **Computerwoche (23 Sep 2024):**
  - No German figures; cites a WSJ/US survey in which 27% of 1,000 HR managers kept postings open four months or longer.
  - Mölleney's reasons: forgotten ads, short-notice cancellations, market scouting and talent pools, legally required ads with an internal candidate, and rarely signalling strength.
  - Warning signs: ads online for months, vague tasks, no start date or deadline.
  - Source: [Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/3535407/immer-mehr-bewerber-sehen-gespenster.html)
- **OnlineMarketing.de (17 Nov 2025):** the "knapp 30 Prozent" headline comes from US JOLTS data (7.4 million openings vs 5.2 million hires, June 2025; a "Phantom Gap" of 28–38% since 2021), via MyPerfectResume. For Germany it says only that the trend exists in "abgeschwächter Form". — [OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/karriere/human-resources/ghost-jobs-jede-dritte-stelle-existiert-nicht)
- **unternehmer.de (25 May 2026):** "Experten schätzen, dass bis zu einem Drittel …" Cited studies: Clarify Capital (1,000 managers; one in five did not plan to fill advertised posts promptly; 10% left posts open six months or more) and StandOut CV (UK: more than a third of 2023 ads were ghosts). No German data. — [unternehmer.de](https://unternehmer.de/wirtschaft/625515-ghost-jobs-jede-dritte-stellenanzeige-betroffen)
- **ingenieur.de (8 Jul 2026):**
  - LiveCareer US survey (918 HR staff, March 2025): 45% regularly post roles not meant to be filled promptly. The article explicitly warns the data do not transfer to Germany.
  - Appinio/Indeed German ghosting survey (Section 6).
  - Warning signs: online for months or reposted unchanged, no publication date, absent from the career site, no contact person, vague tasks and start date.
  - "Eine einzelne … reicht nicht": no single sign is enough.
  - Source: [ingenieur.de](https://www.ingenieur.de/karriere/arbeitsleben/ghost-jobs-wenn-die-traumstelle-nur-ein-koder-ist/)
- **leinetal24 / Ippen (10 Jul 2026):** the headline "Bis zu 40 Prozent aller Stellenanzeigen sind Fakes" has no named source. The body cites Greenhouse (18–22% of positions on its platform at any time) and a JobLeads survey; it was produced in cooperation with Newsweek, with US data. — [leinetal24](https://www.leinetal24.de/verbraucher/ghost-jobs-so-erkennen-sie-fake-stellenanzeigen-im-netz-zr-94391464.html)
- **personalintern.de (6 Aug 2026):** reprises the US LiveCareer data. — [personalintern.de](https://www.personalintern.de/artikel/45-prozent-der-personaler-geben-zu-regelmaessig-ghost-jobs-auszuschreiben/)
- **Other coverage found:**
  - t3n reports Greenhouse's 18–22% estimate. — [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1670553) (excerpt)
  - business-punk (July 2024) also covered the topic. — [business-punk](https://www.business-punk.com/?p=191024) (article page returned 404 when fetched)
  - Austrian outlets (profil.at, leadersnet.at, politjobs.com) also covered it but are out of scope.

### Inferences

- **No German prevalence anchor exists.** Any prevalence prior must be borrowed from foreign data and adjusted. German institutional factors push in both directions:
  - toward more pre-decided postings (BR-firm and public-sector formalities);
  - toward more pool postings (temp agencies, about 20% of BA vacancies);
  - toward fewer fully fake postings (§99 documentation; Konkurrentenstreit).
- **Media warning signs.** The signs media repeat (age, unchanged reposts, missing date, missing contact, absent from the career site) are generic. German-specific refinements come from the earlier sections: occupation and shortage baselines, agency flag, public-sector flag, collective-agreement-aware salary parsing.

### Gaps

- Coverage in the major business press (Handelsblatt, FAZ, SZ, Spiegel, WirtschaftsWoche) and the terms "Phantomstellen", "Scheinausschreibung" and "Stellenanzeigen ohne Einstellungsabsicht" could not be searched separately after the budget ran out. The searches that did run surfaced no German prevalence study.

## 10. Check of Wenske's Germany-specific claims and employer-type patterns (including "1 in 7 to 1 in 3" and senior roles)

### Takeaway

- **Mechanisms with German legal backing:** works-council and public-sector formality, and long notice periods. The formality mechanism concerns pre-decided outcomes, not non-existent jobs.
- **Unverified:** the Probezeit-retention claim, the employer-type patterns (multinationals, Mittelstand, tech) and "senior roles hit disproportionately". No German data was found for any of them.
- **Not supported by German data:** the "1 in 7 to 1 in 3" prevalence range. No German measurement exists, and all figures in circulation are US or UK.
- **Not mentioned by the article (as briefed), but well evidenced:** temp-agency portfolio postings (official BA statement) and never-deregistered postings.

### Cited Findings

- **Formality mechanisms:**

### Inferences

- **Senior ads look ghost-like by construction.** Long durations, no salary and pool-based search are all normal for senior roles in Germany. A naive model will over-flag senior postings. Seniority-specific baselines are needed before any "senior roles are more often ghosts" conclusion can be drawn from data.
- **Suggested German ghost taxonomy for labelling:**
  1. **Pre-decided formality:** BR firms and public sector; real vacancy, outcome fixed.
  2. **Pool or portfolio:** temp agencies (BA-confirmed) and evergreen direct-employer ads.
  3. **Stale or never deregistered:** BA-confirmed.
  4. **Frozen or cancelled after posting:** anecdotal.
  5. **Fully fictitious:** no German evidence of prevalence.

  Each draws on different features. German law makes subtypes 1 and 2 structurally more common than in less-regulated markets, while making 5 riskier for regulated employers.

### Gaps

- The Wenske article itself was not found, so his sources (if any) for the Probezeit claim and the 1-in-7 to 1-in-3 range could not be checked.
- No German employer-type data (multinational vs Mittelstand vs scale-up) on posting behaviour, budget-freeze cancellations or evergreen shares.
- No German data on ghost prevalence by seniority.
