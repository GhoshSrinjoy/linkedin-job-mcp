    Three verdicts

    Every job gets three separate answers.

    • Ghost-risk. Is the position really open and filled from outside?
    • Your chance. If it is, how crowded is it?
    • Communication. Does the employer answer? A rejection counts as an answer; 30 days of silence does not.

    Five patterns

    Why a job may not be really open.

    Possibly filled or abandoned

    The role is filled or dropped, but the ad stays up.

    SignsGone from the employer's own site, far older than usual, start date or deadline long past.

    Candidate pool

    No specific vacancy; candidates are collected for later.

    SignsStaffing agencies, "Talentpool", "Initiativbewerbung", unchanged reposts.

    Internal favourite

    A real vacancy, advertised only because a rule requires it.

    SignsInternal-posting wording, public employer, unusually narrow profile.

    Hiring frozen

    A freeze or restructuring stopped hiring after the ad went up.

    SignsLayoff, insolvency or freeze news while the ad stays online.

    CV collection

    The ad signals growth or collects CVs with no role behind it.

    SignsFar more openings than the company could fill, generic text.

    Data sources

    • Employers' own job feeds: Personio, Greenhouse, Lever, SmartRecruiters, Ashby, Recruitee, Workday
    • LinkedIn, through your linkedin-jobs-mcp
    • Bundesagentur für Arbeit: job board and vacancy statistics
    • Google for Jobs, Adzuna and Arbeitnow
    • GDELT for company news, TED for public contracts, OpenStreetMap for the map

    The radar never logs in to job sites and never stores recruiters' contact details.
