# Lifecycles, not ages, expose German ghost jobs

The best measurable signals for telling German ghost postings apart from slow but genuine hiring are lifecycle anomalies judged against German baselines, not raw posting age. Four of them stand out. The first is how far a job's lifetime, counted across reposts, exceeds the median vacancy duration for its occupation. The second is cosmetic reposting: a new ID or refreshed date with unchanged requirements, on a regular cadence. The third is aggregator copies that stay live after the role has vanished from the employer's own ATS or careers page. The fourth is an ad that outlives its own stated start date. Together these signals carry the most weight when they are rolled up into an employer's track record. No signal has been validated against hiring outcomes anywhere, and Germany has no measured prevalence. Every rate in circulation matches US or UK sources, not a German measurement. That includes the "1 in 7 to 1 in 3" that Bernd Wenske's September 2026 LinkedIn article attributes to unnamed studies. The pipeline therefore has to create its own ground truth through audited samples. German institutions also change the problem. Hiring is slow by design: **86 days from search start to actual start in 2025** ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx)), and **156 days on average** before a vacancy registered with the BA (Bundesagentur für Arbeit, the federal employment agency) is removed from the register ([BA](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/Statistikdaten/Detail/202609/arbeitsmarktberichte/monatsbericht-monatsbericht/monatsbericht-d-0-202609-pdf.pdf)). Works councils and public-sector advertising duties produce real roles whose outcome is already decided. Temp agencies, flagged on **21% of BA Jobbörse ads** ([BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)), openly advertise for client orders they only expect. The dashboard therefore needs labels for each type, not a single verdict. Signals that dominate US tools (missing salary, LinkedIn and Indeed responsiveness badges, "urgent" labels, applicant counts) are weak, misleading or impossible to collect lawfully in Germany. Funding and distress data matter mainly because they explain why an employer's posting volume drifts away from its headcount. The cleanest lawful inputs are employer ATS feeds, schema.org markup on careers pages and BA statistics, with Common Crawl archives and the Arbeitnow and Adzuna APIs as cross-checks. The richer BA Jobsuche backend and the big job boards ban robots in their terms. Naming companies is defensible under German law only as a transparent evaluation of documented, observable facts: "reposted unchanged four times since March", never "fake job".

## Germany has five ghost types and no measured prevalence

"Ghost job" bundles together several phenomena, and each needs its own detector. The US Congressional Research Service defines ghost postings as ads "for positions that do not exist, or that employers are not planning to fill immediately." It lists unintentional causes separately, above all job boards copying ads that are never taken down after the role is filled ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12977)). Economists call a filled job whose ad stays visible a **phantom vacancy**. In the leading directed-search model, the share of listings that are still true vacancies falls as listings age ([Albrecht, Decreuse & Vroman](https://docs.iza.org/dp13704.pdf)). The IAB treats an abandoned search (Suchabbruch) as a genuine failure to fill a role on the terms offered, after which re-advertising on new terms is rational ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/forschungsbericht/2013/fb0613.pdf)). The definitions collide at the edges. Ng counts pipeline ads for specialised roles as ghosts ([Ng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21771)), while LinkUp counts long-running evergreen postings for high-turnover jobs as real openings ([LinkUp](https://www.linkup.com/insights/blog/the-absurd-myth-of-ghost-jobs)).

A definition built on "no genuinely open, externally fillable role" covers all of these. It also adds a German type that US checklists barely mention. The pipeline should therefore give a probability for each type and let each dashboard view apply its own policy. Scams belong in a separate pre-filter. Fees, messenger-only contact and early requests for personal data point to fake employers, whereas ghost postings usually come from real employers through real ATS channels. The only ghost-specific thesis so far shows the cost of mixing the two: by its own sources, two of its three showcased "ghost" postings are a pitch for a paid software course and a subscription scheme ([Mathison](https://commons.emich.edu/context/honors/article/1865/viewcontent/Mathison_Caleb_Honors_Senior_Project.pdf)).

| Type | What happens | German evidence | Traces a pipeline can observe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pre-decided | A real vacancy is advertised because a works council or public-sector rule demands it, but an internal favourite is already lined up | The mechanism is in statute and BAG (Federal Labour Court) rulings; prevalence unknown | Large employer with a works council, or a public body; tailored, rare combinations of requirements; "interne Ausschreibung", reference code (Kennziffer), application deadline (Bewerbungsfrist) |
| 2. Pool or portfolio | No specific vacancy yet; an agency or employer collects candidates for expected demand | The BA states that agency registrations partly serve "Portfoliobildung" | Agency employer or "für unseren Kunden"; "Talentpool", "Initiativbewerbung"; near-continuous presence with unchanged text |
| 3. Stale or never deregistered | The role is filled or abandoned, but the ad stays up or is copied onward | The BA says some employers let ads "durchgehend laufen"; the share of vacancies closed after 1,000+ days is rising | Origin removed while an aggregator copy stays live; lifetime far beyond the occupation norm; stated start date long past |
| 4. Frozen | A budget freeze or restructuring halts hiring after the ad goes up | Only anecdotes in German coverage | Distress events while posting volume stays flat; an isolated posting that is never refreshed |
| 5. Fictitious | The ad signals growth or harvests CVs with no role behind it | No German evidence on prevalence; the works-council paper trail makes it awkward | Postings far above plausible hires; flat headcount; generic text |

### Every headline rate measures something else

The most defensible foreign figure comes from Greenhouse, an ATS vendor. It classified **18–22% of jobs posted on its platform** in any given quarter as ghosts. The figure rests on real ATS activity, but Greenhouse has never published the rule it used ([Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/uk/blog/greenhouse-2024-state-of-job-hunting-report); [ClearStar](https://www.clearstar.net/?p=6218)). Its companion claim, that almost 70% of client companies posted at least one ghost in Q2 2024 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/16/job-market-rife-fake-listing-ghosting-hiring-managers/)), is close to arithmetic. At a 20% rate per posting, a firm with five or six postings has a 67–74% chance of at least one ghost (1 − 0.8ⁿ). Any company-level dashboard metric must remove this volume effect.

Revelio Labs found that US **hires per posting fell from 0.75 in 2018 to below 0.5 in 2023**. It matched each posting to new positions started within six months at the same company, role, seniority and state. The ratio cannot separate ghosts from duplicated postings or slower hiring ([Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/news/macro/ghost-job-postings)). Ng's widely quoted 21% is the share of 269,347 Glassdoor interview *reviews* flagged by a BERT model trained on GPT-4o labels that were never checked by humans. Of its 3,624 keyword hits, 3,169 came from the word "ghosted", which describes silence after applying rather than a job that does not exist ([Ng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21771)).

Employer surveys count respondents, not postings. ResumeBuilder's "40% posted a fake job" is its screener pass rate (649 of 1,641), and its "3 in 10" is 31% of respondents ([ResumeBuilder](https://www.resumebuilder.com/3-in-10-companies-currently-have-fake-job-posting-listed/)). Scrapes based only on age do worst. StandOut CV's UK figure of 34.4% is the share of Indeed listings tagged "30+ days ago". That rule labels **59.1% of veterinary-nurse ads** as ghosts, in what is plainly a shortage occupation ([StandOut CV](https://standout-cv.com/stats/ghost-job-listings-statistics-uk)).

Germany has no measurement at all. Wenske attributes the range of roughly 1 in 7 to 1 in 3 to unnamed "independent studies" ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)). The range matches US and UK figures, not German data. The lower end matches a Clarify Capital analysis, reported as one in seven of 175,000 live US Indeed listings, whose primary source could not be found ([Spectrum News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/nys/capital-region/politics/2026/04/30/n-y--state-senate-passes-bill-to-crack-down-on--ghost-job--postings)). The upper end matches the US and UK one-third figures above. German headlines such as "knapp 30 Prozent" and "bis zu 40 Prozent" trace to the gap between US job openings and hires (JOLTS data), to US surveys, or to no source at all ([OnlineMarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/karriere/human-resources/ghost-jobs-jede-dritte-stelle-existiert-nicht); [leinetal24](https://www.leinetal24.de/verbraucher/ghost-jobs-so-erkennen-sie-fake-stellenanzeigen-im-netz-zr-94391464.html)). A Greenhouse employer survey that included Germany reported only a UK figure and a three-country average ([HRreview](https://hrreview.co.uk/?p=385524)).

Official statistics give outer bounds rather than rates. The share of BA-registered vacancies closed after more than 1,000 days rose **from 0.1% in 2016 to 1.5% in 2025**, which the BA attributes partly to employers who "immer Personal suchen und aus diesem Grund ihre Stellen nicht abmelden bzw. durchgehend laufen lassen" ([BA](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Grundlagen/Methodik-Qualitaet/Methodenberichte/gemeldete-Arbeitsstellen/Generische-Publikationen/Hintergrundinfo-Medianvakanzzeiten-in-der-Statistik-der-gemeldeten-Arbeitsstellen.pdf)). The register also runs ahead of the survey. The IAB vacancy survey estimated 531,900 BA-registered vacancies in Q2 2026, while the BA register held 660,000 in September, so roughly a fifth of register entries may not match a vacancy that firms report as open. That is an upper bound, because the months differ and the BA blames most of the gap on temp agencies ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx); [BA](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/Statistikdaten/Detail/202609/arbeitsmarktberichte/monatsbericht-monatsbericht/monatsbericht-d-0-202609-pdf.pdf)). Postings per real vacancy have also risen. Indeed's German postings index sat about 5% above February 2020 in mid-2026, while IAB survey vacancies were 16% below Q1 2020, which implies 25–41% more postings per real vacancy than before the pandemic (calculated from [Indeed Hiring Lab](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker/master/DE/aggregate_job_postings_DE.csv) and [IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx)). That fits posting inflation, but it fits Indeed gaining coverage just as well. The working rule is to import no foreign prior and to estimate German prevalence only from a weighted audit sample.

### German institutions manufacture pre-decided and pool postings

**Works councils.** A works council (Betriebsrat) can demand that vacancies be advertised internally before they are filled (§93 BetrVG). In firms with more than 20 employees entitled to vote, a missing internal posting lets the council veto the hire (§99(2) Nr. 5) ([§93 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__93.html); [§99 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__99.html)). The BAG requires the internal posting to come before the selection decision and does not allow it to be made up later in court. The employer stays free to pick any candidate ([BAG 1 ABR 16/21](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/1-abr-16-21/)). The statute never requires an external ad. Running internal and external ads in parallel is lawful if both state identical requirements ([stellenmarkt.de](https://personaltrends.stellenmarkt.de/interne-stellenausschreibung)).

The BAG's 2025 chief-physician case shows the resulting pattern. A hospital group advertised a post because its works council demanded it, then chose an internal candidate who already headed another clinic. The council's veto was upheld because the posting fell short of the minimum content the BAG requires: tasks, qualifications and working time ([Otto Schmidt](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/news/arbeits-und-sozialrecht/voraussetzungen-einer-innerbetrieblichen-stellenausschreibung-2026-02-06.html)). The statute's only one-week deadline is the council's window to refuse consent to a chosen candidate (§99(3)). It runs after selection and does not limit how long an ad stays online.

Coverage makes this a big-employer phenomenon. **Only 7% of private establishments with five or more staff have a works council, but they employ 37% of private-sector workers.** 74% of establishments with more than 200 employees have one, against 5% of ICT establishments ([WSI-Mitteilungen 4/2025](https://www.boeckler.de/data/wsimit_2025_04_hohendanner-1.pdf)). §99 also obliges the employer to present the applications to the council before every hire, so a fully fictitious posting leaves an awkward paper trail in these firms. The typical works-council case is a real vacancy whose outcome is already decided.

**Public employers** face stronger duties. Federal posts must be advertised, and publicly when hiring from outside (§8 BBG). Professorships must be advertised publicly and, as a rule, internationally (§45 HRG), and skipping an ad requires the staff council's co-decision (§78 BPersVG) ([§8 BBG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bbg_2009/__8.html); [§45 HRG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hrg/__45.html); [§78 BPersVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bpersvg_2021/__78.html)). Rejected candidates can challenge selections in court under Art. 33(2) GG, and calling off a procedure requires a documented objective reason ([rechtslupe](https://www.rechtslupe.de/verwaltungsrecht/beamtenrecht/rechtsschutz-beim-konkurrentenstreit-und-der-abbruch-des-auswahlverfahrens-336475)). Public postings therefore deserve a higher prior for "pre-decided" and a lower prior for fictitious or evergreen ads.

**Disability law** multiplies formal postings. Public employers must report vacancies to the Agentur für Arbeit (§165 SGB IX). A March 2025 BAG judgment holds that every employer must issue the BA a formal placement order and that a Jobbörse ad alone is not enough. An employer who fails to do so faces a presumption of disability discrimination ([§165 SGB IX](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_9_2018/__165.html); [BAG 8 AZR 123/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-123-24/)). Three consequences follow for the pipeline. BA listings are increasingly compliance registrations attached to real processes. BA copies of public jobs can appear after the employer's own portal, which makes them duplicates rather than reposts. And ads outlive decisions: in that same case, the employer escaped liability by proving that selection had finished before the claimant's application arrived through an ad that was still live.

**Temp agencies.** The clearest official confirmation of speculative postings comes from the BA itself. It says temp-agency registrations are oriented "sehr stark an erwarteten Aufträgen für die Zukunft" and "teilweise zunächst der Portfoliobildung dienen" ([BA Zeitarbeit report](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Statistiken/Themen-im-Fokus/Zeitarbeit/generische-Publikation/Arbeitsmarkt-Deutschland-Zeitarbeit-Aktuelle-Entwicklung.pdf)). Agencies supply about a fifth of BA-registered vacancies while employing 1.8% of the workforce (same source). On 24 October 2026, **214,947 of the Jobbörse's 1,024,646 postings** carried the temp-agency flag, and staffing firms TimePartner and Randstad were among the largest posters ([BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)). The BA leaves agency jobs out of its vacancy-duration statistics because pooling overstates demand ([BA Methodenbericht](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Grundlagen/Methodik-Qualitaet/Methodenberichte/Uebergreifend/Generische-Publikationen/Methodenbericht-Engpassanalyse-Methodische-Weiterentwicklung.pdf)). A German scorer should likewise grade agencies against their own peer group, under a "pool" label.

**Legal status.** None of this is illegal. This research found no German statute, court decision or political initiative that sanctions posting without intent to hire. Applicants have no right to have their applications processed or answered ([unternehmer.de](https://unternehmer.de/wirtschaft/625515-ghost-jobs-jede-dritte-stellenanzeige-betroffen)). Case law under the anti-discrimination act (AGG) polices sham *applicants*, not sham postings ([BAG 8 AZR 21/24](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/entscheidung/8-azr-21-24/)). German immigration rules create no duty to advertise either. The labour-market test is off by default for skilled workers and Blue Card holders, and where it applies the BA runs it internally ([§39 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__39.html)). Salary ranges remain voluntary: Germany missed the 7 June 2026 deadline to transpose the EU Pay Transparency Directive and had published no draft bill by early October ([BT-Drs. 21/7026](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/070/2107026.pdf); [lulius.ai](https://www.lulius.ai/blog/entgelttransparenzgesetz-2026)).

**Foreign disclosure rules** offer a data model a German pipeline can borrow. Ontario has required employers with 25 or more staff to state whether a posting is for an existing vacancy since 1 January 2026 ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/f3b6cad0/ontario-updates-rules-on-job-postings-and-more)). New York's S8877 has passed both chambers and awaits the governor. It would require a statement in bold capitals that the post is a current vacancy with a fill date, a vacancy to be filled no sooner than a later date, or resume collection only, and it would require removal within two weeks of filling ([NY Senate](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8877); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/new-york-severance-agreement-and-job-posting-bills-await-governors-signature)). Texas opened a consumer-protection investigation into LinkedIn over ghost jobs in July 2026 ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/texas-investigates-linkedin-over-alleged-ghost-jobs-targeting-job-seekers)). German ads carry no such self-declaration, so every type has to be inferred.

## Excess lifetime and cosmetic reposting outrank raw posting age

### German baselines turn age into a relative measure

Of the 31 tools, checklists and German-language articles reviewed for this report, 26 use posting age. That makes it the most cited signal, and also the one that transfers worst to Germany. German hiring takes **86 days from search start to actual start** (2025), of which 61 days were planned ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx)). BA-registered vacancies closed between October 2025 and September 2026 averaged **156 days** from the desired start date to deregistration, and 47% had been open more than three months ([BA](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/Statistikdaten/Detail/202609/arbeitsmarktberichte/monatsbericht-monatsbericht/monatsbericht-d-0-202609-pdf.pdf)). Drywall, plumbing-and-heating and building-construction jobs run about 280–300 days ([Statista](https://de.statista.com/infografik/35884/berufe-mit-den-laengsten-vakanzzeiten-von-arbeitsstellen-in-deutschland)), and only 35% of the Jobbörse's stock on 24 October 2026 had been published within the previous four weeks ([BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)). Thresholds built for the US would flag most of that stock. ghostjobdetector.app gives 16 of its 114 points at 30 days ([flags](https://ghostjobdetector.app/api/v1/flags)), JobGhosting calls a single posting ID "stale" at 60 days ([JobGhosting](https://jobghosting.leveragepointadvisory.co/transparency)), and Skip This Job sets minimum scores of 50, 75 and 88 at 60, 90 and 120 days ([Skip This Job](https://www.skipthisjob.com/how-it-works)).

Three sources argue against such cut-offs. Wenske says that US advice of the "3–6 months = fake" kind does not fit Germany ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)). Orbyt concedes that a 30-day rule overcounts slow senior hiring ([Orbyt](https://www.orbytlabs.ai/blog/how-to-spot-ghost-jobs)). US HR staff told LiveCareer that their ghost ads usually come down within a month: 15% within a week, 43% within one to four weeks ([personalintern.de](https://www.personalintern.de/artikel/45-prozent-der-personaler-geben-zu-regelmaessig-ghost-jobs-auszuschreiben/)). A fixed cut-off fails both ways: it catches shortage roles and misses short-lived ghosts.

Age still matters in relative form. On Craigslist, ad ages are spread evenly across an ad's 30-day life, which means ads stay up after the job is filled. Applications pile onto the freshest postings: 47% of applications on the job board Dice go to ads under 48 hours old ([Albrecht, Decreuse & Vroman](https://docs.iza.org/dp13704.pdf)). The BA publishes median completed vacancy durations for each 5-digit occupation code in the German occupation classification (KldB 2010) in its shortage-analysis (Engpassanalyse) download. It moved its standard reporting to medians in 2026 because never-deregistered ads inflate means. Hamburg's mean jumped from 115 to 229 days in one month while the median moved only from 73 to 85 ([BA Engpassanalyse](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Navigation/Statistiken/Interaktive-Statistiken/Fachkraeftebedarf/Engpassanalyse-Nav.html); [BA](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Grundlagen/Methodik-Qualitaet/Methodenberichte/gemeldete-Arbeitsstellen/Generische-Publikationen/Hintergrundinfo-Medianvakanzzeiten-in-der-Statistik-der-gemeldeten-Arbeitsstellen.pdf)). The BA clock starts at the employer's desired start date, not the publication date. These tables therefore calibrate relative differences between occupations and regions, not absolute day counts.

The stronger baseline is the pipeline's own lifetime distribution for each group of comparable postings: same occupation, region, qualification level, source, employer type and quarter. Estimate it with survival methods ([Rodríguez](https://grodri.github.io/glms/notes/c7.pdf)) that handle four complications: right-censoring (the posting is still live when the data ends), left-truncation (it was already live at the first crawl), interval-censoring (it was removed at some point between two crawls) and false removals (it disappeared only because the portal was down). Score each posting by the share of comparable postings that stayed live at least as long, the tail probability S(age | group), and flag values below about 0.05. An accelerated-failure-time model gives an easy-to-read expected median, and a discrete-time hazard model handles inputs that change over time, such as cumulative reposts.

Never inherit vendor truncation. Lightcast expires every ad after at most 120 days ([Lightcast](https://kb.lightcast.io/en/articles/6957446-job-posting-analytics-jpa-methodology)), and research based on LinkUp data drops ads older than 180 days ([Meisenbacher et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01470v1)). Both throw away exactly the tail a ghost detector needs. Even among Fortune 500 firms, 25% of postings run past 90 days and 10% past 180, with differences between employers driving most of the variation (same source). Two German adjustments matter straight away. Academic roles took about twice as long to fill as unskilled ones: 107 versus 53 days in 2015 ([IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2016/kb0416.pdf)). And software development and IT sales lost shortage status in 2025, so a shortage now explains long-open developer ads less well than it did ([BA Engpassanalyse 2025](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Statischer-Content/Statistiken/Themen-im-Fokus/Fachkraeftebedarf/Fachkraefteengpassanalyse/Fachkraefteengpassanalyse.html)).

Wenske explains long German ad lives by saying employers withdraw an ad only after the new hire completes the probation period (Probezeit) ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)). This research found no survey, study or HR-practice source supporting that. The six-month probation with two weeks' notice is real ([§622 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__622.html)). German employers also hedge probation risk through fixed-term contracts used as a "verlängerte Probezeit" ([IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2019/kb1719.pdf)). But nothing shows that they keep ads online for this purpose. The claim is testable: if it were common, the BA would record deregistrations of filled vacancies bunching about six months after the desired start date.

Notice periods are the documented driver of long time-to-start. The gap between the hiring decision and the start date averaged about 35 days for permanent hires in 2018 (same source). The IAB attributes the unplanned part of vacancy durations partly to notice periods in candidates' previous jobs ([IAB-Kurzbericht 4/2016](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2016/kb0416.pdf)). Yet these delays come after the ad has done its work: searches that collect a pool of applications use an application window of about 18 days, followed by roughly 45 days of selection ([IAB-Kurzbericht 17/2019](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2019/kb1719.pdf)).

The sharper staleness cue is an ad that outlives its own stated start date, but explicit start dates are scarce. About 78% of Jobbörse postings show a start date of today or earlier, which suggests that "ab sofort" (immediately) is stored as the publication date ([BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)). No ATS feed exposes an employment start date, and field names mislead: Workday's unofficial feed calls the posting date `startDate`, which matched the "Posted Yesterday" label in live samples ([Workday, Airbus tenant](https://ag.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/ag/airbus/job/Montreal-Area/Leader-Processus-Outillage---Tool-Process-Leader_JR10450490)). Job markup rarely helps either: only **3.3% of German (.de) hosts** that publish schema.org JobPosting markup set `jobStartDate` ([Web Data Commons](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/structureddata/2024-12/quads/classspecific/JobPosting/JobPosting_domain_stats.csv)). The start-date feature must therefore rely on explicit future dates in BA data or on dates parsed from the ad text, such as "zum 01.04.2027".

Expiry dates are more widely available. 57.8% of German JobPosting hosts set the expiry field `validThrough`, and 98.2% set `datePosted` (same source), which Google defines as "The original date that employer posted the job". Google adds: "If the job is filled before the expiration date occurs, remove the job posting". Expired jobs must be marked with a past `validThrough`, a 404/410 or removed markup ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)). Some ATSs carry planned expiry dates as well: Recruitee's `close_at`, d.vinci's `endDate` (probably the end of the publication window) and Greenhouse's `application_deadline`, which was empty for all 39 jobs on one live board ([Recruitee](https://docs.recruitee.com/reference/offers); [d.vinci easy](https://aekno-karriere.dvinci-easy.com/de/jobs/269/auszubildende-kaufleute-fur-buromanagement-mwd); [N26 on Greenhouse](https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/n26/jobs)). Three patterns therefore document staleness or refreshing in the employer's own data: markup still live long after its own `validThrough`, a `datePosted` that moves forward while the job ID stays the same, and expiry dates that keep being pushed back. Board markup deserves less trust. One StepStone page in Common Crawl's October 2026 crawl set `validThrough` about eleven months after `datePosted`. That is a single observation, but it suggests a placeholder rather than a real expiry ([Common Crawl index](https://index.commoncrawl.org/collinfo.json)). New York's pending bill supplies a ready threshold by requiring removal within two weeks of filling ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/new-york-severance-agreement-and-job-posting-bills-await-governors-signature)).

### Repost type matters more than repost count

Reposting is the second most cited signal (24 of 31 sources), yet some reposting is evidence of a genuine search. In the directed-search model, relisting costs money, which signals that a job is still open ([Albrecht, Decreuse & Vroman](https://docs.iza.org/dp13704.pdf)). In an IAB pre-test with 50 HR managers, 44% of the most recent abandoned searches had already been re-advertised ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/forschungsbericht/2013/fb0613.pdf)). LinkedIn pauses free posts after 14 days and closes them after 30 unless they are promoted, so reposting there is routine ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a517777)). Observed rates are modest. 9.9% of LinkedIn postings reappear after a gap of ten or more days ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/ghost-jobs/linkedin)), and only about 2% of Remote Rocketship's live listings had ever been revived ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/advice/ghost-jobs-report/)).

What separates the cases is the type of repost. A cosmetic repost keeps the same employer, normalised title and location, with near-identical requirements and skills (often the same text hash). It comes back under a new ID or a reset date shortly after the previous instance vanished, on a cadence that tracks the portal's posting terms. A new requisition carries a different requisition ID or changed skills, seniority, contract or location, and it follows a long gap after a confirmed close or appears alongside related postings that are live at the same time. Existing trackers already encode versions of this rule. JobGhosting labels a role "ghost" after three or more ID changes for the same normalised title over at least 90 days in total, and keeps evergreen talent-pool roles in a separate class ([JobGhosting](https://jobghosting.leveragepointadvisory.co/transparency)). An Apify scorer adds 15 points per repost under a new ID, capped at 30 ([Apify](https://apify.com/robotninja/ghost-job-detector)). Wenske's view fits the same split: frequent reposting on the employer's own careers page shows ongoing investment, while near-identical cosmetic reposts are a red flag ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)). Even experts could not decide 29 of 176 possible duplicate pairs in a German/English production system ([Engelbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06257)), so the classifier needs an explicit "uncertain" class. Requisition IDs make the split far easier where ATSs expose them. Greenhouse's `requisition_id` (set on all 39 jobs on one live board), Workday's `jobReqId`, SmartRecruiters' `jobId` and `refNumber`, softgarden's `project_number` and d.vinci's `reference` all identify the underlying requisition ([N26 on Greenhouse](https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/n26/jobs); [Workday, Airbus tenant](https://ag.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/ag/airbus/job/hamburg-area/duales-studium-bachelor-of-science-software-engineering--angewandte-informatik---d-m-w-_jr10420411); [SmartRecruiters, Bosch](https://api.smartrecruiters.com/v1/companies/BoschGroup/postings/744000157589077); [softgarden](https://dev.softgarden.de/frontend-v3/jobs-eines-kanals/); [d.vinci easy](https://aekno-karriere.dvinci-easy.com/de/jobs/269/auszubildende-kaufleute-fur-buromanagement-mwd)). Lever and Ashby expose no requisition ID, so their postings need text fingerprinting ([Lever](https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/leverdemo?mode=json&limit=2); [Ashby](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)). Two date fields need care. Greenhouse's `updated_at` changes on bulk edits (nine jobs on that board shared one timestamp), so it is no freshness signal. Whether Personio's `createdAt` or Ashby's `publishedAt` reset when a job is republished is undocumented. The excess-lifetime score must use a job's total live days across all its reposts, not the age of its newest instance, because date resets are exactly what defeat naive age features.

The BA Jobbörse once looked like a shortcut, because it showed first and current publication dates under the same reference number. A 2026 schema change renamed the fields, and the community mapping derives the new `datumErsteVeroeffentlichung` field (first publication date) from the old *current*-date field. Whether the first-publication history survives is therefore unconfirmed ([bundesAPI issue #69](https://github.com/bundesAPI/jobsuche-api/issues/69)). Nor is it known whether the BA reuses reference numbers when an ad is republished. Until repeated snapshots over time settle both questions, repost detection on BA data needs the same fuzzy fingerprinting as every other source.

### Origin removal tests the phantom mechanism directly

The third lifecycle signal tests directly whether a filled job is still being advertised. Copies that boards make of an employer's ad can stay up after the original is removed ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12977)). The BA's own deregistration date lags the actual filling by an amount that "cannot be quantified" ([BA Engpassanalyse](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Navigation/Statistiken/Interaktive-Statistiken/Fachkraeftebedarf/Engpassanalyse-Nav.html)). Aggregator "Just posted" badges reflect data syncs, not job status ([MoreThanCareer.de](https://morethancareer.de/blog/pharma-bloodbath-part-xvii-ghost-jobs-ghost-candidates-german-data.html)). The company homepage was used in **70% of German hires in 2025** and was the decisive channel in 20% ([IAB-Forum](https://iab-forum.de/online-medien-sind-bei-der-personalsuche-auf-dem-vormarsch/)), so a genuine role normally exists on the employer's own channel.

The feature counts the days an aggregator or BA copy stays live after the employer's ATS feed or careers page dropped it. Presence on job boards proves nothing on its own: JOIN multiposts every new job to job boards by default, and SmartRecruiters can send ads to all free aggregators ([JOIN](https://docs.join.com/llms.txt); [SmartRecruiters](https://developers.smartrecruiters.com/llms.txt)). The signal is the copy that outlives its origin. A weaker variant, "never on the employer's own channel", applies only to employers known to run a careers site or ATS feed. Orbyt calls absence from the careers page "the fastest disqualifier there is", and one open-source scorer gives ATS verification a 25% weight ([Orbyt](https://www.orbytlabs.ai/blog/how-to-spot-ghost-jobs); [pulkitchowdry](https://github.com/pulkitchowdry/ghostjobdetector)). None of these has been validated.

Removals must be confirmed: require at least two missed checks in a row, plus a fetch that returns HTTP 404/410, "nicht mehr verfügbar" text or a redirect to a search page. Arbeitnow, for example, answers HTTP 410 for removed postings, as Common Crawl's captures show ([Common Crawl index](https://index.commoncrawl.org/collinfo.json)). A whole feed that suddenly answers 401 or 404 means "no public feed", not that every job closed. SmartRecruiters' documentation now says a token is required even though anonymous calls still work, and Recruitee will reject calls without an employer token from 10 February 2027 ([SmartRecruiters](https://developers.smartrecruiters.com/docs/posting-api); [Recruitee](https://docs.recruitee.com/reference/authentication-1)). The pipeline must also check that each source was crawled completely. A single parser bug at JobGhosting, which stopped reading Workday job boards after 40 openings, created **28,246 false closures across 311 employers** in six weeks ([JobGhosting](https://jobghosting.leveragepointadvisory.co/transparency)).

## Employer context separates frozen roles from slow hiring

The only descriptive evidence with real weight for any ghost signal concerns employers. Across 512,744 listings from 46,517 companies, Remote Rocketship found that **93.2% of companies had no "likely ghost" listings, while 6.8% (3,147) produced all of them**. Flagged listings had been open a median 187 days, against roughly 35 days for a typical company to fill a role ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/advice/ghost-jobs-report/)). The numbers are partly circular, because age makes up 35% of the score that defines "likely ghost". Still, concentration this extreme means an employer's track record carries much of the signal. Useful employer features include the slow-close rate (the share of the employer's closed listings that ran 180 days or more), a careers board whose openings barely changed in 90 days ([Apify](https://apify.com/robotninja/ghost-job-detector)), counts of repeat offences and the median posting score. The pipeline can compute all of these from its own archive of past postings. The academic evidence points the same way: differences between employers drive most of the variation in how long postings stay up ([Meisenbacher et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01470v1)).

### Funding and financial health explain why postings diverge from headcount

Growth predicts fast, genuine filling. In US establishment data, the share of open jobs filled each day is 1–2% at stable establishments but above 10% at those growing 7% or more a month. It falls by more than half from small to large establishments ([Davis, Faberman & Haltiwanger](https://www.econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/Establishment_Level_Behavior_Vacancies_Hiring_QJE_2013.pdf)), and fast-growing firms also recruit more intensively ([Gavazza, Mongey & Violante](https://violante.economics.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf5621/files/documents/gavazza-et-al-2018-aggregate-recruiting-intensity.pdf)). The resulting feature compares an employer's active jobs with its expected hires: headcount growth plus replacement hiring at the sector's turnover rate. An alternative is postings per 100 employees, compared with peers of the same sector and size. Remote Rocketship gives open roles relative to headcount 20 of its 100 points, and found 427 companies advertising more roles than they had employees ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/advice/ghost-jobs-report/)).

In Germany the obvious headcount source is off-limits. LinkedIn's User Agreement bans scraping by any automated means, and after LinkedIn sued the data vendor Proxycurl, the vendor shut down to comply with the settlement ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement); [Proxycurl](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)). The lawful substitute is the average employee number in annual accounts. These are filed with the Unternehmensregister (company register) for fiscal years from 2022, and with the Bundesanzeiger (federal gazette) for earlier years. Small companies must still report the number, micro companies can avoid it, and figures lag one to two years ([§288 HGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hgb/__288.html); [Unternehmensregister ANB](https://www.unternehmensregister.de/i18n-doc/D061_UReg_nutz_0118_de.pdf)). Adjust for the number of locations and branches before comparing. Legitimate multi-site employers dominate raw counts: the discounter Netto alone had 25,482 Jobbörse postings on 24 October 2026 ([BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)). Replacement hiring is also large, since 62.4% of German hires in 2024 came straight from another job ([IAB-Forum](https://iab-forum.de/iab-stellenerhebung-die-meisten-neueinstellungen-gehen-nach-wie-vor-auf-jobwechsel-zurueck/)).

Funding cuts both ways. Wenske treats a funding announcement, expansion language and posts by the hiring manager as reasons to put full effort into an application, and layoffs, freezes or restructuring as reasons to skip ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)). But signalling growth to investors is also one of the motives CRS lists for posting without intent to hire ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12977)). Survey wording decides how big that motive looks. 66% of hiring managers who admitted fake postings to ResumeBuilder picked "appear to be growing" from a list of leading options ([ResumeBuilder](https://www.resumebuilder.com/3-in-10-companies-currently-have-fake-job-posting-listed/)). In Clarify Capital's 2025 survey, only 6% of employers cited it, against 57% who said they were building a candidate pool ([TheJournal.ie](https://www.thejournal.ie/article.php?id=6772517)).

The defensible feature is therefore about timing. A funding round followed by fresh, distinct requisitions lowers risk. A surge of postings around a funding or earnings event that is not followed by headcount growth in the next filing raises it.

Distress is the cleaner signal. About a quarter of US recruiters in one survey said they post to look as if there is no hiring freeze ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2024/08/19/recruiters-posting-ghost-jobs-problem-job-seekers)). 28% of US HR professionals named a position put on hold as a reason for going silent on candidates ([personalintern.de](https://www.personalintern.de/artikel/45-prozent-der-personaler-geben-zu-regelmaessig-ghost-jobs-auszuschreiben/)). An insolvency notice, layoff report or freeze announcement followed by unchanged posting volume is the classic frozen-role pattern. Wenske's lowest effort tier explicitly includes recent layoffs or a freeze.

German data limit what is feasible. Insolvency notices on insolvenzbekanntmachungen.de have no API, cap results at 1,000 hits and are deleted about six months after proceedings end, so the pipeline must query one day at a time and keep its own snapshots ([search info](https://neu.insolvenzbekanntmachungen.de/ap/info_suche.jsf); [§3 InsoBekV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/insobekv/__3.html)). As official notices, they are not protected by copyright under §5 UrhG ([legal notice](https://neu.insolvenzbekanntmachungen.de/ap/rechtlicheHinweise.jsf)). Mass-layoff notifications under §17 KSchG go only to the Agentur für Arbeit and are never published ([§17 KSchG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kschg/__17.html)), so layoffs must come from news. The news database GDELT is free with attribution ([GDELT](https://www.gdeltproject.org/about.html)), and the layoffs.fyi tracker covers tech companies only ([layoffs.fyi](https://layoffs.fyi/)). For funding, startupdetector derives German rounds from the commercial register (8,000+ rounds since 2014, at €385–695 a month) ([startupdetector](https://startupdetector.de/); [pricing](https://startupdetector.de/pricing)), whereas Crunchbase's data terms forbid public display unless the order form allows it ([Crunchbase](https://data.crunchbase.com/docs/data-access-terms.md)).

### Department clusters count only when they are coherent

Wenske's seventh signal says that clusters of related openings in one department suggest active recruiting, while an isolated posting may be frozen. No other of the 31 sources reviewed raises it, and it seems to contradict a widely cited warning against many openings without visible growth ([Heidelberg24](https://www.heidelberg24.de/verbraucher/ghost-jobs-so-erkennen-sie-fake-stellenanzeigen-im-netz-zr-94391464.html); [NachhaltigeJobs](https://www.nachhaltigejobs.de/geister-jobs/m); [Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/advice/ghost-jobs-report/)). The conflict disappears if the feature measures coherence rather than volume. A coherent cluster contains several *distinct* requisitions, with different requisition IDs, different seniority or skill sets, a shared department and recent first-publication dates, which looks like a funded team build. A volume pattern contains near-identical copies of one posting across cities, or a careers board that has not changed in months.

The ATS feeds make this computable. Personio exposes `department`, Lever `team` and `department`, SmartRecruiters `department`, and Ashby `department` and `team`. Greenhouse returns departments when the request includes `content=true` ([Personio](https://personio.jobs.personio.de/xml); [Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [SmartRecruiters](https://api.smartrecruiters.com/v1/companies/smartrecruiters/postings?limit=1); [Ashby](https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/ashby?includeCompensation=true); [Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)). One caution: a single ad can legitimately cover several vacancies, for example across locations ([Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Methoden/WISTA-Wirtschaft-und-Statistik/2018/05/internetgestuetzte-erfassung-052018.pdf?__blob=publicationFile)). Near-identical copies therefore need a multi-vacancy check before they count against the employer. Because the signal is untested, it should enter the score at a low weight until audit data calibrate it.

### Applicant counts and platform badges mislead more than they inform

Applicant volume is the signal US tools read most directly from LinkedIn. Skip This Job adds 8, 10 or 15 points at 100, 200 or 500+ applicants, and 16 points for a post over 30 days old with 200+ applicants ([Skip This Job](https://www.skipthisjob.com/how-it-works)). On its own the signal is ambiguous, because a popular role also attracts hundreds of applicants. It is also impossible to collect lawfully at scale in Germany. The BA API has no applicant field, no StepStone counter was found, and LinkedIn bans automated collection. The only route is data contributed by users from their own browsing with their consent. That still sits uneasily with LinkedIn's terms and needs a GDPR consent design and review by a lawyer.

Where counts exist, read them against IAB baselines. German employers received **16 applications per hire in 2025 (up from 9 in 2022)**, and only about four of those were suitable. IT and natural-science services drew 40 applications per hire and personal services 8; firms with 250 or more staff drew 27, against 8 at firms with fewer than 50 ([IAB-Forum](https://iab-forum.de/iab-stellenerhebung-2-2026-fast-drei-von-vier-bewerbungen-sind-aus-betrieblicher-sicht-ungeeignet/)). Many applications combined with a long relative age point to a pipeline or ghost posting. Few applications combined with a long age point to a genuinely hard-to-fill role; employers reported "too few applicants" as a difficulty for 18.6% of hires in 2025 ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx)).

Platform responsiveness labels count the wrong things. LinkedIn's „Bewerber:innen werden aktiv geprüft“ ("actively reviewing applicants") appears when a recruiter has "reviewed" an application in the past two weeks, and a review includes an automatic or bulk rejection, viewing a profile or downloading a CV ([LinkedIn Hilfe](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1655396?lang=de); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a1655396)). Indeed's „Aktiver Arbeitgeber“ ("responsive employer") requires a response rate above 51% over 30 days, and automated rejection emails count even after the listing has closed ([Indeed Help](https://www.indeed.com/help/employers/articles/what-is-an-employer-response-badge)). A frozen role with automatic rejection rules can earn both badges, so their presence is weak evidence. Their absence says more, for example „Noch keine Antwort verfügbar“ ("no response insights yet") on a post 14 or more days old. Indeed's „Dringend gesucht“ ("urgently hiring") label comes with paid Sponsored Jobs ([Indeed für Arbeitgeber](https://de.indeed.com/arbeitgeber/recruitingloesungen/attract)). LinkedIn's verification badge confirms who the poster is, not whether the job is open ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1492056)).

Silence reported by applicants is just as weak. **63.5% of employees in Germany who applied in the previous 12 months got no response at least once** ([ingenieur.de](https://www.ingenieur.de/karriere/arbeitsleben/ghost-jobs-wenn-die-traumstelle-nur-ein-koder-ist/)), so non-response makes a poor training label.

### Salary and ad text route postings to types

Missing salary is the heaviest single flag in one popular US checker (18 of 114 points) ([ghostjobdetector.app](https://ghostjobdetector.app/api/v1/flags)), and it means almost nothing in Germany. Only 12% of German Indeed postings stated pay in May 2026 ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/de/blog/2026/05/14/vollstaendige-gehaltstransparenz-ist-in-europa-noch-zukunftsmusik/)), and 24.1% of all positions counted by Index Research in H1 2026 did ([Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=206404)). Software-development ads disclosed pay 6.7% of the time ([Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/3843807/geheimniskramerei-um-it-gehalter.html)), and ads for the most senior levels around 11–12% of the time ([Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=206404)).

The feature should therefore measure missing pay relative to what is normal for the segment. It carries information where disclosure is common: cleaning (41%) and transport (37%) ([Indeed Hiring Lab](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/de/blog/2025/03/05/gehaltsangaben-bleiben-in-deutschland-die-ausnahme/)), hourly roles, staffing agencies (about 40%) and public employers. It carries none for IT, engineering or senior roles. Collective-agreement and civil-service pay grades such as "TVöD E 13", "TV-L E 11" or "A 13" must be parsed as pay information, or employers bound by such agreements will be mis-scored. Transposing the Pay Transparency Directive will not automatically change this, because Art. 5 lets employers give pay before the interview instead of in the ad ([afa-anwalt](https://www.afa-anwalt.de/news/entgelttransparenzgesetz-2026-rl-2023-970/)). The feature needs a switch tied to the date Germany's final law takes effect and to its wording.

Text signals are better at sorting postings into types than at detecting ghosts. Evergreen wording ("Talentpool", "Initiativbewerbung", "für zukünftige Positionen") marks an open pipeline posting, which existing trackers keep as a separate class ([JobGhosting](https://jobghosting.leveragepointadvisory.co/transparency); [Apify](https://apify.com/robotninja/ghost-job-detector)). ATS feeds carry such entries too: a Personio feed checked for this report listed a standing "Initiativ bewerben (m/w/d)" position ([ottonova on Personio](https://ottonova.jobs.personio.de/xml?language=en)). Exclude these entries from lifetime baselines and repost statistics, which they would otherwise inflate. Google's rules treat them strictly. Its job-posting policy counts postings "with the primary purpose of collecting information about applicants, rather than seeking to employ these applicants" as fake, and states that "Publishers may solicit resume collections for open positions only" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)). A pool posting that carries JobPosting markup therefore conflicts with a published standard. Agency signatures, such as "für unseren Kunden", "Arbeitnehmerüberlassung", "Übernahmeoption" or references to the GVP or iGZ collective agreements for temp work, route a posting to the pool type. Internal-candidate markers route it to "pre-decided": "interne Ausschreibung", "Versetzungsbewerber", "Beförderungsdienstposten", or a reference code with an application deadline. Unusually narrow requirement profiles point the same way. Wenske, Karrierebibel and NachhaltigeJobs, the only sources to raise hyper-specific requirements, note that such ads are often real vacancies with an internal favourite ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/); [Karrierebibel](https://karrierebibel.de/pro-forma-ausschreibung/); [NachhaltigeJobs](https://www.nachhaltigejobs.de/geister-jobs/m)). Rarity can be measured by weighting each skill by how rarely it appears, the technique that best separated true duplicates in a German/English production system ([Engelbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06257)).

Works-council and disability boilerplate ("Schwerbehinderte werden bei gleicher Eignung bevorzugt") marks a formal, documented process, which slightly raises "pre-decided" and lowers "fictitious". Vagueness and length features need a validated German word list ("abwechslungsreiche Tätigkeit", "dynamisches Team"), and they must run after legal and employer boilerplate is stripped, or they measure template length rather than effort. The only ghost-specific vagueness list is in English ([Mathison](https://commons.emich.edu/context/honors/article/1865/viewcontent/Mathison_Caleb_Honors_Senior_Project.pdf)). Specificity, such as a concrete start date, interview steps or team context, lowers risk in Wenske's and Orbyt's checklists.

No text signal has been linked to hiring outcomes. One trap is systematic: senior ads look ghost-like by construction. They involve longer searches, rarely disclose salary, use pool-based selection, and executives are exempt from the internal posting duty. Seniority needs its own baselines before anyone concludes that senior roles are more often ghosts.

### Wenske's checklist survives once translated into features

Wenske's 1 September 2026 article is the only source that turns German works-council language, start-date details and clusters of openings into explicit signals. Its claims hold up unevenly ([Wenske](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/decoding-ghost-job-how-spot-frozen-roles-german-market-bernd-wenske-vys6e/)).

| Wenske signal or claim | What the evidence shows | Pipeline translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Posting age is weak in Germany because ads stay up through the Probezeit | IAB and BA durations confirm that German hiring is slow; the Probezeit explanation is unverified | Relative age counted across reposts; test for deregistrations bunching six months after the start date |
| Frequent reposting on the careers page signals investment; cosmetic reposts are a red flag | Consistent with theory (relisting costs money) and with IAB data on re-advertising | Repost-type classifier with an "uncertain" class |
| A named hiring manager posting about growth | A positive signal in only 2 of 31 sources; LinkedIn data off-limits | Manual enrichment during the audit, not an automated feature |
| Freeze, layoff, restructuring or funding news | Practitioners agree; no tool checks news | Distress and funding events joined to trends in posting volume |

| Works-council or statutory posting language | The statute confirms the mechanism; such boilerplate also marks documented processes | Raises "pre-decided", lowers "fictitious" |
| Start dates, interview timelines, probation details | Supported by 5 of 31 sources | Specificity features; overrun of an explicit start date |
| Clusters of related openings versus isolated posts | Only Wenske raises it; conflicts with "volume without growth" warnings | Measure department coherence, not raw volume |
| Patterns by employer type (multinational, corporate with works council, Mittelstand, scale-up) | Works-council mechanism partly confirmed; the rest unverified | Priors based on works-council coverage by size and sector |

| Cosmetic repost | Reappearances within 180 days with near-identical requirements, a new ID or reset date, and a regular cadence | ATS requisition IDs (`requisition_id`, `jobReqId`, `refNumber`, `reference`); `datePosted` compared with first sighting; never Greenhouse `updated_at` | ↑ High if unchanged and periodic; ↓ if content changes after a gap | Widely cited (24 of 31); theory says some reposting is genuine |
| Origin-removal lag | Days an aggregator or BA copy stays live after the employer feed dropped the job (two confirmed misses; a 401/404 on the whole feed is not a closure) | ATS feeds plus sightings on the BA or aggregators | ↑ High (stale type) | Mechanism documented (CRS, BA); not validated |
| Overrun of expiry or start date | Days live beyond a past `validThrough` or planned ATS expiry; repeated extensions; days beyond an explicit stated start date | `validThrough` (58% of .de markup hosts); Recruitee `close_at`, d.vinci `endDate`; start dates from BA data or ad text (`jobStartDate` on 3% of hosts) | ↑ Medium–high (board `validThrough` can be a placeholder) | Follows from definitions (BA vacancy clock, Google policy) |
| Employer slow-close rate | Share of the employer's closed jobs that lasted longer than 90% of comparable postings; careers board unchanged for 90 days | Own archive | ↑ Medium–high, adjusted for small samples | Strong concentration across employers (descriptive) |
| Postings versus expected hires | Active jobs ÷ (headcount growth + headcount × sector turnover), compared with peers | ATS counts; employee numbers from annual accounts | ↑ Medium when far above, especially with flat headcount | Link between growth and fast filling validated; use for ghosts only suggestive |
| Distress with steady posting | Insolvency, layoff or freeze news followed by unchanged or rising posting volume | Insolvency notices; GDELT; ad-hoc company news | ↑ Medium (frozen type) | Practitioners agree; untested |
| Funding with coherent hiring | A recent round followed by distinct new requisitions; a surge without later headcount growth flips the sign | startupdetector; news | ↓ Low–medium (↑ for a surge without growth) | Wenske; signalling motive listed by CRS |
| Department coherence | Distinct requisitions open at the same time in one department, with different seniority or skills | ATS `department` and `team` fields | ↓ Low until validated; an isolated, unchanging posting ↑ | Single source (Wenske) |
| Agency and pool markers | Temp-agency sector code (NACE 78), „für unseren Kunden“, „Arbeitnehmerüberlassung“, „Talentpool“, standing „Initiativbewerbung“ entries in ATS feeds; BA `zeitarbeit` and `pav` flags | Text; sector code in company registers | Routes to the "pool" type with its own baseline; excluded from lifetime statistics | Official BA statement; Google's rule on resume collections |
| Pre-decided markers | Employer has a works council or is public; „interne Ausschreibung“, reference code; rare skill combinations | Text; employer type; skills weighted by rarity | Routes to the "pre-decided" type | Statute and case law (mechanism); prevalence unknown |
| Missing salary | Missing relative to the segment norm; collective-agreement and civil-service grades count as pay | Text; BA `verguetungsangabe`; ATS pay fields | ↑ Low, and only where disclosure is common | 12–24% disclosure makes it weak |
| Applicant volume × age | Applicants per day at a high relative age | Not lawfully available at scale | ↑ if many applicants and old; few applicants point to hard-to-fill | Suggestive (IAB applications per hire) |
| Platform responsiveness labels | Presence or absence of LinkedIn and Indeed badges | Platforms ban scraping | Presence ↓ weak; absence after 14+ days ↑ weak | Automatic rejections count as responses |
| Vagueness and specificity | Vague phrases per 100 words after boilerplate removal; concrete start date, team, tasks | Text with a validated German word list | ↑ / ↓ Low | No evidence on outcomes |
| Scam markers | Fees, messenger-only contact, early requests for personal data | Text | Separate filter, kept out of the ghost score | Fraud literature |

## Build on employer feeds and BA baselines, not scraped boards

### Each German source trades richness against legal exposure

**The BA Jobsuche backend** is the richest single source, and also the most restricted. The BA offers no official jobs API. Its Jobbörse backend, documented by the community project bundesAPI, answered on 24 October 2026 with a fixed public client key. It held **1,024,646 postings**, with first-publication date, last-modified timestamp, start date, salary type, flags for temp agencies and private recruiters, and a stable code identifying each employer ([bundesAPI](https://github.com/bundesAPI/jobsuche-api); [BA Jobsuche](https://rest.arbeitsagentur.de/jobboerse/jobsuche-service/pc/v6/jobs?size=1&page=1)). Results come sorted oldest-first, with a cap on the number of pages ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/jobsuche); [bundesAPI issues](https://github.com/bundesAPI/jobsuche-api/issues)). A daily census would therefore need many split-up queries plus around a million detail calls.

That is exactly what the BA's terms of use (version of 8 July 2026) forbid. §2a(3) bans "Robots, Webspider oder ähnliche Technologien" and reading out interfaces "zum Zweck der Datensammlung und -auswertung". The terms apply to all visitors, and §4 reserves the right to block users and claim damages ([BA Nutzungsbedingungen](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/nutzungsbedingungen)). Older search endpoints already return HTTP 403, detail pages carry no contact data, and the 2026 schema change broke the community documentation. The workable routes are written permission or a research cooperation, with the IAB as the natural partner; BA aggregate statistics; and small, rate-limited validation samples that store no personal data.

**Employer ATS feeds** are the cleanest base for both origin presence and exact dates, and their legal risk is low because they are published for public use. Eight systems common in Germany exposed job data without credentials on 24 October 2026, though not always through a documented API:

Recruitee's careers API will reject calls without an employer-generated token from **10 February 2027**, although its XML job-board feed is not covered by the token ([Recruitee](https://docs.recruitee.com/reference/authentication-1)). SAP SuccessFactors, rexx, Umantis and onlyfy remain largely unverified, and onlyfy job pages carry no dates at all ([onlyfy](https://aithyra.onlyfy.jobs/en/job/16jg1teu9fdy5d88oo7pe4t9ko1jy3t)). No ATS feed exposes an employment start date or flags staffing agencies. Salary appears only where the employer has enabled it (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Recruitee, softgarden).

Vendors publish no terms or rate limits for automated reading, although Lever's documentation states that published jobs "may be scraped by third parties" ([Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api)). The move towards mandatory tokens shows that "public" endpoints can close, so the pipeline should prefer documented endpoints and monitor HTTP status. Feeds also carry personal data that must be stripped on ingestion, such as the recruiter named in SmartRecruiters' detail records ([SmartRecruiters, Bosch](https://api.smartrecruiters.com/v1/companies/BoschGroup/postings/744000157589077)).

Finding the tenants is a task of its own. Feed URLs follow predictable per-company patterns, and the Common Crawl URL index lists tenant URLs for smaller ATS domains, though queries on large domains such as `jobs.personio.de` timed out ([Common Crawl index](https://index.commoncrawl.org/collinfo.json)). Coverage figures exist only as vendor-side claims. TheirStack counts 1,344 German companies on Personio, undated and with an undisclosed method ([TheirStack](https://theirstack.com/en/technology/personio)), and JOIN claims more than 20,000 customers ([JOIN](https://join.com/)). No existing ghost tracker reads Personio or any other German ATS ([Apify Job Lifecycle Monitor](https://apify.com/gganbukim/job-lifecycle-monitor)), which leaves the German Mittelstand uncovered.

**Careers pages** without a known ATS can be read through schema.org JobPosting markup: `datePosted`, `validThrough`, `jobStartDate`, `identifier`, and `baseSalary` rather than the third-party `estimatedSalary` ([schema.org](https://schema.org/JobPosting)). Check robots.txt and any machine-readable opt-out from text and data mining first. Adoption is now measurable. Web Data Commons extracted markup from the October 2024 Common Crawl and released the data in January 2025: 63,320 hosts published JobPosting markup on 3,606,092 URLs ([Web Data Commons](http://webdatacommons.org/structureddata/2024-12/stats/schema_org_subsets.html)). Hosts under .de numbered 9,138 (14.4%, second only to .com), with 275,740 marked-up postings. Among platforms serving the German market, join.com (4,803 marked-up postings), softgarden.io (1,811), arbeitnow.com (530), stellenanzeigen.de (436) and personio.de (223) appear, while stepstone.de, linkedin.com and glassdoor.de are absent ([per-host statistics](https://data.dws.informatik.uni-mannheim.de/structureddata/2024-12/quads/classspecific/JobPosting/JobPosting_domain_stats.csv)). No extraction newer than October 2024 exists, so adoption in 2026 is unknown. Stale markup is not a tooling gap: Google offers employers a fast channel for announcing removals, an Indexing API restricted to job-posting and livestream pages ([Indexing API](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/indexing-api/v3/quickstart)).

**Common Crawl** offers history without crawling. Its index listed 129 crawls on 24 October 2026. Pages can be located through its CDX index and fetched with HTTP range requests on the archive files, although the index API often returned 502 or 504 errors ([Common Crawl index](https://index.commoncrawl.org/collinfo.json)). The October 2026 crawl (CC-MAIN-2026-43, 5–18 October) contains StepStone and XING job pages with JSON-LD markup. One XING example carried `utm_campaign=germanpersonnel` in its URL, showing that it arrived through a multiposting distributor, so tracking parameters can reveal cross-posting routes. LinkedIn, Glassdoor, Careerjet and Indeed job pages are absent because those sites block Common Crawl's crawler. That biases any rate computed from the archive towards sites that allow it. Common Crawl's terms leave third parties' rights intact ([Common Crawl terms](https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use)), so a board's pages inside the archive raise the same legal questions as the board itself.

**The big job boards** are where duplicates and repost labels live, and where collection is riskiest. StepStone's robots.txt prohibits "any other use of robots" and disallows search results and URLs with query strings, though not job detail pages. Its terms pages could not be retrieved, so their scraping clause is unverified ([StepStone](https://www.stepstone.de/robots.txt)). Indeed disallows job-view pages for generic crawlers ([Indeed robots.txt](https://de.indeed.com/robots.txt)). Its terms, updated on 29 September 2026, prohibit bots, scrapers and AI agents without written permission, and its old Publisher API host no longer resolves ([Indeed terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)). LinkedIn's robots.txt ends with a blanket "Disallow: /" for all crawlers ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/robots.txt)). Its Job Posting API is a write interface that is not accepting new partners ([LinkedIn Job Posting API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/talent/job-postings/api/overview)). No German board shows a repost or responsiveness label. StepStone's documented labels, „Schnelle Bewerbung“ and „Anschreiben nicht erforderlich“, describe how much effort applying takes ([StepStone](https://www.stepstone.de/e-recruiting/hr-wissen/recruiting/bewerbung-leicht-gemacht)).

**Open aggregator APIs** are thin but usable as cross-checks. Arbeitnow's API needs no key, updates hourly and returns company, title, description, location, tags and a `created_at` timestamp ([Arbeitnow](https://www.arbeitnow.com/api/job-board-api)). Its jobs come mostly from ATSs such as Greenhouse, SmartRecruiters, JOIN, Teamtailor, Recruitee and Comeet, and its terms ask only that users not abuse the free API and link back ([Arbeitnow blog](https://www.arbeitnow.com/blog/job-board-api)). It carries no salary, start date or expiry, and it leans towards English-language tech jobs. Batches of identical timestamps suggest that `created_at` records ingestion rather than publication.

Adzuna's API covers Germany ([Adzuna API spec](https://developer.adzuna.com/swagger/spec/test2.json)). Each ad carries `created` ("The date the advertisement was placed"), `salary_min`, `salary_max` and a `salary_is_predicted` flag. It also carries an `adref` that can be used to re-retrieve the ad "to confirm that the advertisement is still current", which makes it a sanctioned way to check whether an ad is still live. Its history endpoint returns only monthly average salaries, not posting histories. Personal and academic research use is permitted, but "commercial analysis" requires a written agreement, and results must be attributed to "The Adzuna API" ([Adzuna terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service)). Predicted salaries must never count as "salary stated".

**Baselines** are the easy part. BA statistics may be stored, reproduced and shared with attribution ("© Statistik der Bundesagentur für Arbeit"), provided your own calculations are labelled as such ([BA Statistik](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Home/Service/Impressum/impressum_node.html)). A pilot BA statistics API has served regional vacancy stock and inflow since December 2025 ([BA Statistik API](https://statistik.arbeitsagentur.de/DE/Navigation/Service/API/API-Start-Nav.html)). The IAB vacancy survey and Indeed's German postings index (licensed CC-BY) provide the economy-wide comparison figures ([IAB](https://doku.iab.de/arbeitsmarktdaten/IAB-Stellenerhebung_Indikatoren.xlsx); [Hiring Lab](https://github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker)).

**Company data** is legally tight. The Handelsregister (commercial register) portal limits use to 60 searches or company views per hour and bans systematic retrieval that builds parallel registers, though users with a demonstrable need can apply to have their IP address whitelisted ([Handelsregister](https://www.handelsregister.de/rp_web/information/welcome.xhtml)). It suits looking up individual companies on demand, not mirroring. Bundesanzeiger report pages are protected by captcha, which must not be bypassed ([bundesAPI](https://github.com/bundesAPI/deutschland/blob/main/src/deutschland/bundesanzeiger/bundesanzeiger.py)). OffeneRegister offers CC-BY data dumps of uncertain freshness ([OffeneRegister](https://offeneregister.de/)). North Data licenses API access from €500 a month, with no resale and mandatory attribution ([North Data](https://www.northdata.de/_data); [AGB](https://www.northdata.de/_agb)). Reviews of the application experience have no lawful bulk source: kununu and Glassdoor offer no API, and Glassdoor was legally merged into Indeed on 1 July 2026 ([Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/employee-experience/glassdoor-folds-into-indeed/)). ### Deduplication that keeps reposts is the core engineering task

Duplicates outweigh every other data problem. Textkernel's Jobfeed counted 8.89 million German online job ads in 2014, which shrank to 4.18 million after removing duplicates; each offer appeared in 2.2 sources on average ([Crosswater](https://crosswater-job-guide.com/archives/48931/)). Textkernel estimates that the average ad is reposted two to five times ([Textkernel](https://www.textkernel.com/learn-support/blog/online-job-postings-have-many-duplicates-but-how-can-you-detect-them-if-they-are-not-exact-copies-of-each-other/)). Commercial deduplication destroys the ghost signal. Lightcast, for example, merges postings with the same normalised title, company and location within 60 days and keeps the first one as the "original" ([Lightcast](https://kb.lightcast.io/en/articles/6957446-job-posting-analytics-jpa-methodology)).

The pipeline should therefore keep three levels of data: raw sightings (source, URL, fetch time, HTML hash), posting instances (the lifetime of one URL or ID on one source) and job clusters (all instances of the same job across sources and over time). It should link records rather than delete them, so that the number of reposts, the length of the gaps and the cadence become features of the cluster. The Eurostat deduplication challenge has a "temporal duplicate" class, meaning the same job retrieved on different dates. That class is the formal equivalent of a repost ([deduplication challenge](https://github.com/Dim10p/deduplication-challenge)).

The published evidence supports a matcher built in layers. It starts by stripping boilerplate for each employer (cookie banners, portal navigation, repeated "Über uns" blocks) and hashing the normalised text to catch exact copies. It then compares texts with MinHash/LSH on four-character chunks, which cope with long German compound words, and it only compares postings from the same employer, language and description-length band, as Insee's challenge team did ([Insee](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/Webinar%20Deduplication.pdf)). Matches should be decided with skill-based scores, because whole-text similarity is not enough. True duplicates can share as little as 37% of their text chunks ([Textkernel](https://www.textkernel.com/learn-support/blog/online-job-postings-have-many-duplicates-but-how-can-you-detect-them-if-they-are-not-exact-copies-of-each-other/)), while one employer's boilerplate makes different jobs look alike. In a German/English production system, text overlap alone scored an F1 of only 0.55, while a combined skill-based score mixing skill overlap, skill embeddings and skills weighted by rarity scored **0.94**; in production a pair counted as a duplicate at a combined score of 0.6 or more ([Engelbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06257)). For German–English pairs, translating first and then comparing embeddings with expert rules on top raised F1 for reposted copies to 0.92, against 0.57 for a multilingual model ([Pasch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13695)). When grouping matches into clusters, chain them together only through high-confidence matches ([Insee](https://cros.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/Webinar%20Deduplication.pdf)).

German text needs specific handling. Normalise gender tags such as "(m/w/d)", "(w/m/d)", "(all genders)", "*in" and ":in" before any title matching, and repair tokens that scraping glues together, such as "m/w/dStellentyp" ([Engelbach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06257)). Detect the language of each paragraph, because German ads often wrap English task descriptions, and strip legal and process boilerplate before computing vagueness or length. Language models adapted to the job domain help. GBERT and GELECTRA are strong German baselines ([Chan et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.coling-main.598/)), and further training on job ads consistently improves results and holds up better as wording changes over time ([Gnehm et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2022.lrec-1.414)). jobGBERT carries a non-commercial licence ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/agne/jobGBERT)), while the MIT-licensed JobBERT-v3 matches job titles across German and English ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/TechWolf/JobBERT-v3)). Titles must also be mapped to KldB 2010 codes to join the BA baselines; this research did not evaluate any automated German KldB coder.

### Score transparently, adjust employer grades for sample size, audit everything

Until audited labels exist, nothing in this field supports calling a score a probability. None of the tools reviewed publishes precision, recall or a test against hiring outcomes. GhostCheck itself calls its output "a likelihood score, not a guarantee" ([GhostCheck](https://ghostcheck.app/)). The scorer should start as a transparent, documented weighting that borrows the sound design rules already in use: unknown data counts as neutral, young listings are capped (Remote Rocketship caps listings under 21 days at a score of 25), and no single signal triggers a flag on its own ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/advice/ghost-jobs-report/); [PeerPush](https://peerpush.com/p/jobghosting)). The output should be a probability for each type (stale, pool, pre-decided, frozen) plus a separate scam flag. The dashboard should display a coarse "ghost-risk indicator" grade rather than a single number. Once audit labels arrive, replace the hand-set weights with a calibrated model.

Employer grades need an adjustment for small samples, because a firm with three postings and one flag is not a 33% offender. A beta-binomial empirical Bayes estimate pulls small employers towards the average of their peers: (α + flags) / (α + β + postings), with α and β fitted for each size band and sector. The dashboard should publish coarse grades with uncertainty rather than rankings. Kline, Rose and Walters showed that a four-grade report card keeps the chance of mis-ranking a random pair of firms near 5% ([Kline, Rose & Walters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13005)). Staffing agencies belong in a separate peer group, for the same reason the BA leaves them out of its own durations. Avoid company-level metrics such as "has at least one flag" entirely, since they mostly measure posting volume.

Ground truth has to be built, because every available label measures something different. Reviews measure the candidate's experience ([Ng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21771)), and matching postings to hires measures conversion within a time window ([Revelio Labs](https://www.reveliolabs.com/news/macro/ghost-job-postings)). ATS activity data is proprietary, and an ad's removal does not mean the job was filled ([LinkUp](https://www.linkup.com/use-cases/closed-duration-of-full-stack-jobs)). Confirmation from the employer comes closest to the definition, though employers can be expected to give the socially acceptable answer.

The validation design follows from that. Use the Eurostat job-vacancy definition as the target: a vacancy is a post the employer is actively trying to fill from outside, immediately or within a set period ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/cache/metadata/Annexes/isoc_sk_oja_esmsip2_an_1.pdf)). Combine imperfect labels in a label model, such as a repost after a close, extreme excess age and an origin-removal lag, and treat unlabelled postings as unlabelled rather than as genuine. Then review a stratified audit sample drawn with probability proportional to posting age. Eurostat proposed this design, with the company contacted to check whether each scraped vacancy is really open ([Beręsewicz & Pater](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/documents/3888793/12287170/KS-TC-20-008-EN-N.pdf/6a86d53e-d0b8-d608-988d-d91f0cef6c21?t=1611673495829)). MoreThanCareer's practical test makes a good audit script: ask the named contact in writing whether the headcount is approved and what the target start date is ([MoreThanCareer.de](https://morethancareer.de/blog/pharma-bloodbath-part-xvii-ghost-jobs-ghost-candidates-german-data.html)). Report precision at a fixed review budget and calibration curves, not accuracy.

Two traps follow from earlier sections. Applicants not hearing back is common in Germany even for genuine jobs, so it makes a poor label. And fixing the prevalence in advance produces circular models. The EMU thesis labelled the top 20% of a hand-weighted score as ghosts, and its BERT model reached about 81% accuracy, against 80% for simply guessing "real" every time ([Mathison](https://commons.emich.edu/context/honors/article/1865/viewcontent/Mathison_Caleb_Honors_Senior_Project.pdf)).

## German law permits scoring evidence, not alleging intent

### Collect from published feeds, copy only to analyse, never get around blocks

**Database right.** Bulk copying of job boards is the riskiest activity, because it repeats the facts of CV-Online v Melons. There, the EU Court of Justice held that a specialised job search engine copying and indexing a substantial part of a job-ad database "extracts" and "re-utilises" it. The database maker may prohibit this where it puts the return on its investment at risk ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2021/06/10/database-battle-between-job-ad-websites-eu-court-of-justice-decision-102h08y)). In February 2026 the regional appeal court OLG Köln applied this to a streaming platform that had pulled in the ARD Mediathek. It held that **public funding does not exclude database protection**, and that taking over metadata in bulk over several days counts as repeated, systematic extraction ([OLG Köln 6 U 75/25](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/koeln/j2026/6_U_75_25_Urteil_20260227.html)). That reasoning plausibly extends to the BA Jobbörse.

**Text and data mining.** The exception in §44b UrhG allows copies made for automated analysis, provided they are deleted once no longer needed. It applies only if the rightholder has not opted out in machine-readable form ([§44b UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__44b.html)). In the LAION case, the regional appeal court OLG Hamburg held that a ban written in plain language in terms of use was not machine-readable by 2021 technology. It hinted that commercial or permanent use could be decided differently, and an appeal to the Federal Court of Justice (BGH) is reportedly pending ([CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/vervielfaeltigung-eines-fotos-fuer-erstellung-eines-ki-trainingsdatensatzes-zulaessig)). For databases, a contract can exclude the commercial data-mining exception but not the research exception ([§87c UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__87c.html)). The research exception covers only non-commercial operators that have no private sponsor with decisive influence and preferential access to results ([§60d UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__60d.html)). Neither exception covers publishing the copies. Formal opt-outs are rare in practice. A check of the sites discussed here on 24 October 2026 found no TDMRep file, TDM header or TDM meta tag, only robots.txt rules aimed at AI crawlers ([Indeed robots.txt](https://de.indeed.com/robots.txt)). Whether such rules count as a valid reservation under §44b(3) is legally unresolved. Common Crawl does not change the analysis. Its terms leave third parties' rights intact ([Common Crawl terms](https://commoncrawl.org/terms-of-use)), so a board's pages taken from its archive raise the same database-right questions as scraping the board directly.

**Terms of use and technical barriers** set the hard limits. The BGH held in 2014 that automatically reading freely accessible data despite click-accepted terms is not in itself unfair competition, but that overcoming technical protections could be ([LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/hintergruende/h/bgh-urteil-izr22412-screen-scraping-flugdaten-automatisiert-auslesen-ryanair-reiseportal)). Bypassing access controls risks criminal liability under §202a StGB, and getting around technical measures that protect a database risks liability under §95a UrhG ([§202a StGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__202a.html); [§95a UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/__95a.html)). The pipeline must never solve captchas, rotate IP addresses to evade blocks, use logins or borrow other parties' API keys. The BA, LinkedIn, StepStone and Indeed expressly ban robots or scrapers, and Adzuna requires a written agreement for commercial analysis. Greenhouse and Lever, by contrast, publish their feeds for public use, and Arbeitnow asks only for fair use and a link back ([BA](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/nutzungsbedingungen); [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement); [Indeed terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal); [Adzuna terms](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service); [Greenhouse](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [Lever](https://github.com/lever/postings-api); [Arbeitnow](https://www.arbeitnow.com/blog/job-board-api)).

**Data protection** is manageable. "Legitimate interest" under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR is a workable legal basis for scraping public data. The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) and the OLG Köln, in a case on Meta's AI training, both accepted it with safeguards ([EDPB Opinion 28/2024](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf); [OLG Köln 15 UKl 2/25](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/koeln/j2025/15_UKl_2_25_Urteil_20250523.html)). The EDPB lists respecting robots.txt as one such safeguard. In practice, the pipeline should strip recruiter names and contact details when data comes in, including ATS fields such as SmartRecruiters' `creator.name` and softgarden's `job_owner_*`. It should also publish a list of sources and a channel for objections. It should also handle sole-trader employers ("Praxis Dr. X", firms registered as e.K.) with care: the GDPR protects natural persons, these business names identify one, and scoring them is therefore profiling of a person ([GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).

**The EU AI Act.** Its high-risk employment category covers systems that recruit or select people, target job ads at them, or filter their applications ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)). A scorer of postings and employers built for job seekers most likely falls outside it. That changes if a job board uses the score to target ads or to make decisions about individuals. The Digital Omnibus regulation moved the high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 ([NI Assembly](https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/assembly-business/brexit-and-beyond/eu-law-scrutiny-tracker/regulation-2026-1744/)). Summaries written by an LLM and published on matters of public interest must be disclosed as AI-generated unless a human has reviewed them editorially ([AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)).

### Publish observable facts and frame scores as evaluations

Naming companies turns on the line between opinion and factual claim. Under §824 BGB, anyone who spreads an untrue fact that damages another's creditworthiness is liable for damages ([§824 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__824.html)). Companies' personality rights are weaker than individuals' and must tolerate more criticism, but they still protect against untrue factual claims ([WBS](https://www.wbs.legal/medienrecht/persoenlichkeitsrecht/unternehmenspersoenlichkeitsrecht)). Four lines of case law set the template. On ratings, the BGH treats a credit-rating number as a value judgement based on facts. It becomes a factual claim only when the evaluative element fades behind the facts, and claims against it fail if the opinion rests on accurate facts ([BGH VI ZR 120/10](https://rewis.io/urteile/urteil/ixg-22-02-2011-vi-zr-12010/)). On algorithms, the BGH upheld Yelp's algorithmic filtering of reviews because readers understood how the average was computed ([Plutte on BGH VI ZR 496/18](https://www.ra-plutte.de/bgh-plattform-yelp-bewertungen-selektieren-aussortieren/)).

Product-testing case law sets the quality bar. An evaluation is normally lawful if it is carried out "neutral, sachkundig und im Bemühen um objektive Richtigkeit" with a "vertretbar" (defensible) method, and in the leading case the manufacturer had seen the test programme and its results in advance ([LG München I](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Pdf/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2023-N-1236?all=False)). Rating-portal case law sets the process duties. The doctor-rating portal Jameda lost its protection as a neutral intermediary by favouring paying customers ([Otto Schmidt](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/news/wirtschaftsrecht/arztebewertungsportal-jameda-bgh-bewilligt-loschungsanspruch-mangels-neutraler-informationsvermittlung-2018-02-20.html)), and an objection from a rated business triggers a duty to verify ([LTO on BGH VI ZR 1244/20](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/bgh-vi-zr1244-20-online-bewertung-hotel-darlegungslast-kontakt-kunden-gaeste)). If the operator competes with those it rates, as a job board, ATS vendor or recruiter would, the unfair-competition rule in UWG §4 Nr. 2 puts the burden of proving truth on the operator ([§4 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__4.html)). For a commercial operator, publishing other job boards' "ghost shares" is therefore the riskiest output.

The main risk is the word itself. "Ghost job", "Geisterstelle", "Fake-Stelle" or "Scheinausschreibung" can read as the factual allegation that an employer deliberately advertises jobs it will not fill, and intent is close to impossible to prove. Even "80% probability this job is fake" risks reading as a statement of suspicion. For those, German courts require a minimum stock of supporting facts, no prejudgement and, normally, a chance for the named party to comment ([WBS](https://www.wbs.legal/medienrecht/botschafter-geht-gegen-spiegel-und-mdr-vor-bgh-schaerft-voraussetzungen-zulaessiger-verdachtsberichterstattung-68672/)).

The safer design shows timestamped observations first and the operator's evaluation second. Published platform rules make good yardsticks for those observations. Google bans expired job postings, requires filled jobs to be removed, and counts postings whose main purpose is collecting applicant data as fake ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting)). A statement such as "markup still live 40 days after its own `validThrough`" therefore records a provable departure from a documented standard without imputing intent. It publishes the method and the known patterns behind false positives (evergreen pools, retail chains with many sites, staffing agencies, apprenticeship cycles), and it applies the method identically to all employers. It runs a complaint-and-reply process, notifies employers before naming them for the first time, and never sells employers a way to improve their score. It publishes rates by sector, region and occupation by default, and names employers only when the evidence and observation period pass set thresholds. It leaves small firms and sole traders unnamed, and it never republishes kununu or Glassdoor review text. No German court has ruled on algorithmic ghost-job labels, so a media lawyer should review the wording and process before launch.

| Reads as an allegation (avoid) | Reads as a documented evaluation (prefer) |
| --- | --- |
| "Ghost job", "Fake-Stelle", "Scheinausschreibung" | "Reposted with unchanged text 4× since 2 March 2026" |
| "This company has no intention to hire" | "Open longer than 95% of comparable postings (occupation, region, quarter)" |
| "80% probability this job is fake" | "Not found on the employer's careers page on 20 and 22 October 2026" |
| "Collects applicant data" | "Ghost-risk indicator (our evaluation): high. Criteria and data below" |
| "Breaks Google's job-posting rules" | "Job markup still live 40 days after its own `validThrough` date; Google's guidance requires expired postings to be removed" |

## Conclusion

The evidence changes the question from "is this job fake?" to "what kind of non-fillability is this, and how sure are we?" In Germany, most postings that fail the open-and-externally-fillable test are lawful side-effects of institutions: formal postings required by works councils and public-sector rules, agency portfolios, and ads that nobody deregisters. The BA itself documents the last two. This framing works better technically, because each type leaves its own observable traces. It is also safer legally, because each label then rests on facts that can be proven rather than on an assumed intent. The main lever is not a clever text classifier, since no text signal has been linked to outcomes. It is infrastructure that tracks postings over time: full lifetimes tracked across employer feeds and aggregators with no cut-off, deduplication that keeps reposts, survival baselines for each occupation, and employer histories adjusted for sample size. No existing tool provides this for German job boards, German ATSs or German text.

The binding limits are ground truth and data access, and one step addresses both. The richest data source, the BA Jobbörse, bans robots in its terms, while the IAB vacancy survey already asks establishments whether they are actively searching. A research cooperation with the BA and IAB would unlock both the postings and the first German validation labels. It could also test the open questions directly, from Wenske's Probezeit claim to the rising share of vacancies closed after 1,000 days. Until then, every published score is an evaluation still waiting to be calibrated. The pipeline should also be built to absorb changes in the rules. Germany's eventual pay-transparency law, New York-style removal duties on platforms and the BA's switch to median durations will each shift the baselines. ATS vendors' moves towards mandatory access tokens, such as Recruitee's from February 2027, can close data sources the pipeline depends on. Switches tied to the date each change takes effect, and regular re-calibration of baselines, belong in the design from day one.
